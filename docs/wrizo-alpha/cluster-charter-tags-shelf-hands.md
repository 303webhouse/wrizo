# THE CONNECTED-BOARD CLUSTER — charter (Fable's desk, 2026-09-02)

CLAIM: items 115, 117, and 119 are ONE SYSTEM wearing three
numbers, and designing them separately would build three
different answers to the same question — how does writing
CONNECT across surfaces?

115 (tags) is the DATA of connection: a card carries metadata; a
tag is a link a writer can search from Draft without an AI call.
117 (the board shelf) is the GEOGRAPHY of connection: a page
linked to boards, boards as thumbnails in the Tools Menu, cards
opening in popups, edits syncing both ways. 119 (mirrored hands)
is the GRAMMAR of connection: both hands anchored to every
writing surface — page, board, card — so the same two-handed
posture follows the writer everywhere. The shelf's popup card IS
a writing surface (119's coverage clause); a tag search from
Draft lands ON the shelf (117's geography carrying 115's data);
the hands must mount on what the shelf opens. One committee pass,
three outputs, one geometry.

SCOPE: full committee (Experts why · Architects how · Marketing
opposition on reach-vs-complexity), double-pass. BINDING INPUTS:
Nick's three verbatim charters (the Westeros example whole; the
import-viewer note; the mirrored-hands sentence); the
canvas-inside-fixed-frame measurement; item 83's geometry laws;
the anti-bogged-down posture Nick attached to 115 AND 116
("maintaining progress, not ironing out every minor detail").
EXPLICIT NON-GOALS: Storyboard/Outline formatting (noted, not
designed); item 116's import mechanics ride BEHIND the cluster
(the viewer is its own small charter once the shelf exists).
OUTPUT: one design document with a ticketed build order, to Nick
for his word. The pass runs at Fable's desk; drafts land as
records commits.
