## CHAMBER A — the fresh Board's beginnings (SV3, deep)

**Experts — why.** BM1 ruled boards born empty, and the data ruling
was right; the sitting proved the *experience* is a dead end — a
writer walks through a door into an unfurnished room with a sign
pointing at tools that don't mount. The empty state must be the room's
own furniture: the anti-blank-page thesis, applied to the Board. And
these are doors, not tasks — no checklist, no progress, nothing
counted; they vanish the moment the board has furniture, because an
invitation repeated becomes a nag.

**Architects — how.** A centered "beginnings row" of quiet olive
icon+label doors, generous spacing, no tile grid (the grid is the
State); rendered only when the board has zero boxes — no engine
needed, presence follows the data. Capped at five doors per mode; the
full set lives in the Board's LHP forever after. Per mode:

- **OPEN:** New Card · New Page Card (births a linked page — the
  Thread's ruling 6, arriving early) · Load a Deck (the house word for
  "preset" — the seven-deck library) · Connect a Page (pin an
  existing one) · Pin a Reference (see the seal, below).
- **STORYBOARD:** Load a Deck leads (decks are the storyboard's native
  furniture) · New Lane · New Card.
- **OUTLINE:** New Card · Load a Deck.
- **A paired plan board:** OPEN's row exactly; a "seed from the page"
  door was considered and deferred to the Thread committee — it walks
  too near the ghostwriter's chair to rule in passing.
- **System boards (Journal/Shelf/Trash): no beginnings row, ever.**
  Derived membership; their empty states are quiet declarative lines,
  never invitations. You do not "start" a Trash.

**The Import question — Tension 2, named, not smoothed.** Marketing's
opposition: "Import Doc on an empty board reads as *bring your old
manuscript in* — the exact first move this app exists to refuse."
Experts' counter: writers pin research; a corkboard that refuses all
clippings is a toy. **Resolution proposed — the Reference Seal:**
imported docs and images land only as sealed reference cards —
visually clipped/pinned, viewable in place, and with NO path into any
page: not insertable, not copyable-in, the A13 grammar extended to
imports as a permanent structural rail. The seal's mechanics are their
own ticket; until it exists, the Import/Upload doors do not ship. The
row launches with the first four doors; Pin a Reference joins when the
seal does.

## CHAMBER B — the Two Hands (SV5, deep)

**Experts — why.** This universalizes an emergent truth into grammar:
the left hand is the desk (tools the writer wields), the right hand is
the counsel (things that speak when spoken to). The Page already lives
this way — tool strip left, Tutor right. SV5 makes it law for every
surface. And one sentence before any table: **every RHP on every
surface is born inside A12–A15.** Extending counsel everywhere
multiplies the ghostwriter's attack surface; the constitution is not
renegotiated per surface — it is inherited whole, including the
never-writes, never-inserts, nothing-sent-unless-asked rails.

**Architects — the grammar, then the table.** One drawer family for
every mount: FX10's engine, the `data-open` contract, the vanishing
law, hover-restore — one engine, N surfaces (the projection law's UI
cousin). The floating arrows die into this: an arrow is only ever a
drawer's handle, rendered by the one component, olive at rest.
Proportionate mounting — Marketing's opposition ("a card with two
full-height drawers is chrome inflation") is sustained: on small
surfaces the two hands mount as the two EDGES of the existing popup;
the popup becomes the card's desk. Suggested house names for Nick's
taste: **the Desk side** and **the Counsel side** (LHP/RHP remain the
spec terms).

**The table — committee suggestions for Nick's ratification, not
final law:**

- **Prose Page.** Desk: the July-17 ruled strip — word-processing
  first (bold, italic, strikethrough, size, alignment, indent, fonts
  with the deliberate custom-font door), the Typewriter cluster
  (Forward Momentum · Text Fade · Page Scroll) under the typewriter
  icon, Progress Bar with placement options; per-mode variation per
  the second-sitting chambers already awaiting ratification. Counsel:
  the Tutor as built — Talk it through, Consistency, Structure (in
  Thread language; the beats sentence dies), Fragments (deduplicated),
  the Bible; the nudges return here, later, under content law.
- **Script Page.** Desk: element cycling (scene/action/character/
  dialogue/transition), scene navigation, the script's own per-mode
  strip. Counsel: the Tutor with script lenses — continuity and
  name-consistency, which his own embedded testing asked for by name.
  Details belong to the SC arc.
- **Board.** Desk: the beginnings set, permanently (New Card, New Page
  Card, Load a Deck, Connect a Page, Pin a Reference when sealed);
  lane management in STORYBOARD; nesting controls in OUTLINE. Counsel,
  programmatic first: the Map (cards linked to no page; this project's
  unpinned pages — LISTED, never counted), empty-lane notes, a
  connections view; conversational counsel on the board is deferred —
  **what board content travels to the model is a disclosure-sentence
  question (v4 territory), and no board text rides until that sentence
  is ratified.**
- **Card.** Desk (as the popup's left edge): rename, color, links —
  the Thread made visible: linked pages listed, each a jump,
  link/unlink; send to trash. Counsel (the right edge), programmatic
  only in v1: where this card lives, its siblings, its linked pages —
  no per-card AI in v1; the smaller attack surface is the point.
- **Ink / Image.** Desk: pen, clear, the existing ink affordances.
  Counsel: programmatic only — where it's pinned. **And one rail
  stated for the record: Wrizo never generates images. The app never
  holds the pen in any medium.**

the arcs (Naming, Hands, Pagination, Publish, HB2) need Nick's call on
vacation-critical versus post-vacation.
