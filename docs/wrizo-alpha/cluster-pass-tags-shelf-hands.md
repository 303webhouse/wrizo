## §0 · THE ONE QUESTION
How does writing CONNECT across surfaces? 115 answers with DATA
(a card carries names a writer can search), 117 with GEOGRAPHY
(a page knows its boards; a board's cards open from the page),
119 with GRAMMAR (the same two hands follow the writer onto
every surface). Designed apart, these are three answers; designed
together they are one corkboard with pins — which is what the
Westeros writer actually wants.

## FIRST PASS

THE EXPERTS — why one system. A writer connecting a family tree
across boards is not managing a database; they are pinning cards
and drawing threads. The moment tags become TYPES, hierarchies,
or required fields, the corkboard becomes a schema and the writer
stops. So the law of the cluster: TAGS ARE NAMES, STRUCTURE COMES
FROM LINKS. A tag is a word the writer typed; hierarchy is the
thread between two cards, labeled by the tags they share. Search
is lookup, never inference — Nick's "without having to run an AI
check" is the architecture, not a preference: the Consistency
lens already reads entry.boxes locally with no wire, and the tag
census inherits that exact posture. Tags do NOT reach the Tutor
in this pass — that is a wire change and belongs to item 108's
disclosure work.

THE ARCHITECTS — the mapping onto what exists. (1) THE LINK
ALREADY EXISTS: a board PINS a page (fw2's pin relation, in
entry.boxes) and a page has a plan board (the planBoardId
pointer). "Link this page to a board" IS pinning the page there —
no new relation, zero schema; a page's shelf = its plan board +
every board pinning it. (2) THE CARD POPUP IS A WRITING SURFACE:
the opened-card dock is already the board's edit view; edits are
the same row, so "sync both ways" is free by IDENTITY, not by a
sync engine — there is one card, seen from two places. (3) THE
SHELF IS A CASCADE: item 83's Desk drawer already cascades
leftward (CascadePanels); the board thumbnails live in the Tools
drawer, and a board's card thumbnails are one cascade level
further — the geography reuses the grammar. (4) THE HANDS ANCHOR
TO THE BOX THE WRITER SEES: the paper on pages; on boards the
canvas WRAP, not the canvas (measured: the wrap's edge is what
holds still while the canvas scrolls inside it — anchoring to
the canvas would chase a scrolling target); on the card popup,
the popup itself. Anchors are layout; script measures only. (5)
ONE DRAWER, TWO MOUNTINGS: the mirror is structural — a single
drawer implementation with a side prop, so fade, slide, announce
and coexistence cannot drift between hands (E3's reuse-never-copy
law made permanent by construction; the existing S3 timing guard
generalizes to a geometry mirror assertion). (6) THE 1100 FLOOR:
the −29.7px overhang is the anchor law failing at the frame's own
minimum width; surface-anchoring closes it, and the probe now
visits 1100 to prove it.

THE OPPOSITION (Marketing / simplicity). Objection: the shelf is
a lot of chrome for a feature most writers reach late. Answer:
every ticket below is independently shippable and walkable; the
first two are pure quality (the hands finally coherent, the
overhang gone) and cost the writer nothing; tags are a text field.
Objection: a tag census invites taxonomy creep — filters, types,
colors. CONCEDED AND MADE LAW: the census is a flat list of names,
autocomplete only; no tag types, no colors, no required tags, no
hierarchy field. Structure is threads. Objection: "search from
Draft" risks a second navigation system. Answer: it lands in
Draft's existing Find section (item 83 lists it), results open in
the dock — one door, already designed.

THE WORKING-WRITERS BENCH. The Westeros writer wants to SEE the
tree, not administer it: pin a card, draw a thread, type "Stark"
in the box at the card's foot. From a Draft page, search "Stark"
and every Stark card appears; open one and it's the same card,
editable, with both hands on it. The bench's one warning: if
tagging feels like filing, nobody tags — so the Tags box is
free-text at the card's foot, chips beside it, autocomplete from
what the writer already typed elsewhere, and nothing else.
