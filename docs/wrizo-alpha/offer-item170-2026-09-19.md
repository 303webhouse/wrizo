## §2 · S0 — EVERY WAY A WRITER COULD OPEN AN EXISTING PAGE

**Pages could be opened. That is the finding, and it is why the report is right
anyway.**

| path | opens a page? | the catch |
|---|---|---|
| Journal panel → **Recent** | **opens** | **capped at 5**, and Journal pages only |
| Journal **survey** | **opens** | Journal pages only (`inJournalView`) |
| **Drawers** panel tiles | **opens** | binder docs, plus loose docs via the Shelf law |
| a board card, **double-click** | **opens** | only pages already pinned on *that* board |
| **Boards connected** (PW1) | opens **boards**, not pages | — |
| **Page hand's list** ("Place page on board") | **places only** | see the four faults below |
| Tutor fragment → its source | opens | niche; reached from a fragment, not a list |

Every path that *does* open a page is named for **where a page lives** — the
Journal, a Drawer, a board — and **none of them lists all of a writer's pages.**
The one list in the app that looked like "your pages", sortable by Date · Drawer ·
A–Z, did the opposite of what it looked like.

**THE OLD LIST'S FOUR FAULTS — and a relabel would have fixed only the first:**

1. **Clicking a row PLACED the page** on the board instead of opening it.
2. **It existed only on a board.** `PagePanel` mounted it under `onBoard`, so a
   writer standing on a page had no list of pages in that hand at all.
3. **It could not see the pages writers make most.** It read `getJournalPages()`,
   and `inJournalView` returns **false for every `origin: 'loose'` page** — which
   is exactly what **New Page** and the **Desk** create. The newest page was
   missing from the list most likely to be looked in.
4. **It capped at 30 before it sorted.** `.slice(0, 30)` ran first, so A–Z
   ordered thirty pages rather than all of them, and the rest were unreachable by
   any sort.

**No harness touched this list at all** — zero coverage — so no park is owed, and
nothing was ever holding its behaviour in place.

## §3 · WHAT WAS BUILT — AS FABLE RULED IT, AND THREE JUDGMENTS FOR VETO

**Clicking a row OPENS the page.** `flushNow` first, as every door that leaves a
surface does, then `routeForEntry`. **Placing on a board is the row's secondary
act**, behind its `⋯`, and appears **only where there is a board to place onto** —
on a page it is absent rather than present and inert.

**One derivation for the row:** `itemTitle`, the Journal lists' own, so a page is
called the same thing here as everywhere else it is listed. The home label now
tells **Journal** from **Loose**, because the widened population mixes them and
the old single "Loose" fallback would have mislabelled every Journal page.

**THREE JUDGMENTS, each flagged for Nick's veto:**

1. **The list is on EVERY surface, not only boards.** "The way to open a page"
   that exists only while standing on a board is not the way. I read the ruling
   strongly here.
2. **The population is every live page that is not a board** — journal, loose,
   binder and shelf alike. Boards are excluded: they have their own doors and can
   never be placed on a board, so neither act applies to them. **If Nick expects
   boards in this list, that is a one-line change.**
3. **The heading is "Your pages" — a noun, against a live precedent.** The lexicon
   cites S13: *"a bare noun list in a drawer reproduces the GO-versus-PUT
   confusion."* That confusion came from a noun heading over a click that **put**.
   The click now **goes** — which is what a noun list promises — and the put is
   named by its own verb, "Place on this board". So I read S13 as satisfied, not
   overridden. **The strongest case for the rival** ("Open a page", a verb phrase):
   it honours S13 to the letter and names the primary act outright. I chose the
   noun because the list serves two acts, and a heading naming one of them
   misdescribes the other.

## §4 · HOW IT WAS CHECKED — INCLUDING WHAT THE CHECKS MISSED

**RED BEFORE: 12/14 on the old code.** But that alone did not prove what it
appeared to. On the old code the list is **absent** from a page surface, so the
population and cap checks failed *because there was no list*, not because of the
faults they are named for. And one check — **"placing does not navigate" —
passed on the old code with nothing placed at all.** Both were fixed so each
check can only fail for its own reason.

**ONE MUTATION PER FAULT, against the committed new code:**

| mutation | what failed |
|---|---|
| **F4** cap before sort | exactly **FAULT 4 + the A–Z check** |
| **F1** click places | exactly the **two "click opens" checks** |
| **F3** population | **FAULT 3 — and not FAULT 4** (the cap check is scoped to Journal pages, the population the old list could see, so it cannot be failed by the population fault); S3's place checks fail downstream, since the loose row is the one S3 places |
| **F2** board-only | FAULT 2 and every page-surface check, as expected — it removes the surface they read |

**THE SITTING CAUGHT WHAT ALL FOURTEEN CHECKS MISSED.** On the board, the last
row's `⋯` lit up as expanded and **no menu appeared**: it hung below the row, and
the list's own `overflow-y:auto` clipped it out of view. Every check passed
through it, because the harness's click **scrolls its target into view first** —
precisely the move a writer would not know to make.

- **Fixed and turned into a check (S4)** that reads the menu's geometry **without
  scrolling to it**, on the last row with the list scrolled to its end: the
  writer's view, not the harness's.
- **S4 then caught my own second cut.** I centred the menu on the row; S4
  measured a **3px overhang** (`menuBottom 501` against `listBottom 498`) because
  the menu was taller than the row. I would have called that fine by eye. The
  menu is now **pinned to the row's top and bottom**, so its height derives from
  the row's and cannot exceed it.
- **S4 fails on both earlier versions and passes only on the last.** A second
  sitting confirms it by eye.

The menu also closes on **Escape or an outside press** — before, the only way to
dismiss it was to find the `⋯` again.
