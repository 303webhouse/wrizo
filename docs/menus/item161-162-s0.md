**Finding 1 — the panel overlays the board at EVERY width the app can have**, by 150–384px, because the margin beside a 1100px board never reaches the panel's 320px minimum (the cap is 310 at 1720). And **finding 2 — that is why Nick saw the tab "overlapped":** the closed tab sits at the board's edge, and when the panel opens it spreads 150–384px *inward* from the stage edge, so **the tab ends up in the middle of the panel it opens** (z-index 7 keeps it painted above the panel, but it floats on the panel's face with the board's edge running through it).

## 161 — THE FIX (CSS only; `index.css`)

**While the panel is open, the tab TRAVELS WITH IT** — it sits on the panel's own near edge (the joined-tab shape `.wz-tutor-panel{border-left:none}` was always drawn for), so the panel **cannot cover it wherever the panel lies**. Closed, the tab is flush to the board's edge exactly as before. The board never moves (the panel is absolute — page primacy holds). Docked, the tab rides the docked panel's edge (`clamp(120px, margin, open-w)`).
- `.desk-frame-tutor-anchor--board:has(.wz-tutor-grip[data-open='true'])` → `width:16px; right:var(--tutor-panel-open-w)`; the docked variant reads the docked clamp. **`:has()` reads the grip's own `data-open` / `data-docked`** (Tutor.tsx already writes both) — no JS, no new prop.
- **`--tutor-panel-open-w` moved to `.desk-frame-stage`** (one declaration, two readers: the panel's width and, on a board, where its tab sits) — the FX10 S1 comment's own rule ("declared ONCE so two rules never drift").
- **Board only.** Prose and screenplay measure their margin in JS (FX18 S2) and stay flush to the paper.

**The rival, in its strongest form — SHRINK THE PANEL TO THE MARGIN** (keep the tab at the board's edge, make the panel start outside it): it covers nothing and needs no travelling tab. **Its cost, measured above:** the margin is 52–310px against a 280px usable floor, so the panel would be **unusably narrow (or absent) below ~1900px viewports.** *The unmeasured risk sits on the fix I built:* a tab that moves when the panel opens is a change in muscle memory; nobody has watched Nick use it.

## ⚠ HANDED UP — the 166 amendment's "always enough room on either side of the board" cannot hold on a board at any width this app has

Nick's Q2: *"There should always be enough room on either side of the board, though, for the strip menus to open without the board being moved or overlapped."* PLAN DESK's §3 reads that as **a static gutter each side**, "the board sized to the stage MINUS both gutters", with the board's floor `CANVAS_MIN_W` 560. **Measured:** a Tutor gutter (460 + the 28px gap = 488) **plus the Tools-dock gutter on the other side** leaves the board `stage − 2 × 488`; at 1366 that is **310px, below the 560 floor**, and it only reaches 560 at a **stage of ~1536, i.e. a ~1620px viewport.** So **"always" is unreachable on any laptop-class screen** — which is exactly the guideline's own *"if they can't … let's have the menus just overlap the board."* **161's fix therefore takes the overlay branch and does what the guideline's safeguard asks; it does NOT try the static-gutter reading.** *(If Nick's "always" is meant literally on laptops, the price is a board narrower than its own floor — his call, with these numbers.)*

## 162 — THE FIX (CSS only)

Item 137 pinned Themes/Trash to the strip's foot, but the strip was only `min-height:70vh`, so the foot floated wherever 70vh ended (mid-screen on any tall window). **`.desk-frame-strip` gains `bottom:-3rem`** — the host's own bottom padding (`.desk-frame-host{padding:… 3rem …}`); `--fx3-chrome-budget` already states the host's height is the viewport's (40 + 63 + 16 + stage + 48 = `100vh`), so the strip's bottom edge **is** the screen's. Taken from the host's own padding rather than `100vh − N`, so it follows the layout instead of a second hand-synced constant. `min-height:70vh` stays as a floor. Item 130's `z-index:1` and item 137's flex column are untouched; the strip stays `position:absolute` at x=0.

*Unmeasured, and named:* this assumes the host's bottom **is** the screen's bottom — true whenever the page is not scrolled and the stage is above its 360px floor. On a viewport short enough to hit that floor the host exceeds the screen and the strip follows the host (the document bottom), not the screen edge. The harness measures 768 / 900 / 1200 at 1280; a 600px-tall window is not in it.
