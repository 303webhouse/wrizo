The last ride

The snow came in sideways, the way it always did when the old roads remembered they had been rivers. Ned rode ahead, which was his habit, and said nothing, which was his other habit.

Catelyn had written three letters that morning and sent none of them. The first asked him to stay. The second asked him to take the boys. The third said only that the crypt was cold and that she had been down there again.

By the ford the horses would not cross. He dismounted and led them, water to the knee, the current stronger than it looked, and when he turned to call back to the others the words did not come.

There is a kind of refusal that looks like stubbornness from the outside and is something else entirely from within: a man keeping a promise to a person no longer there to release him from it.
