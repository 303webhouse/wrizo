## §0 · THE OFFER IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Free Write becomes what Nick's analog law says it is: **a typewriter for text
and a journal page / sketch pad for drawing.** A **TEXT | INK** switch sits in
the band; in INK the caret sleeps, an ink stratum wakes over the paper, and the
Tools drawer reveals **TIP · NIB · INK · eraser** — absent, never greyed, in
TEXT. The Journal's whole J-series drawing pipeline ports across. **STYLING
leaves Free Write**, and so does the older zone that was *called* "Ink" but in
fact coloured the typed text. **Zero schema, zero server, zero network.**

## §3 · THE GATE ANSWER — ZERO SCHEMA, NO STOP

`strokes` is an existing **`jsonb` column in `journal_entries`' original table
body** (`migrate.ts:56`), and `journal_entries` is the single row-per-page table
every page kind lives in — `pageType` sits on the same interface as `strokes`
(`types/index.ts:189`, `:203`). Three optional keys **inside** that blob are
invisible to Postgres: no DDL, no backfill, no migration.

**Checkable, and checked:** `git diff 2b8e5b4..HEAD -- apps/server packages` is
**empty**. This wave touches no server file at all, which is why it carries no
server-behaviour clause and needs no ship word of that kind.

## §4 · WHAT WAS SURFACED, NOT BUILT

### STOP — held for Nick's word
1. **Flux's four ink values.** No ruling or canon names an ink for any theme but
   Plateau, and TH2 explicitly declined to give Flux a pen ink at all — *"Journal
   ink is its own sealed domain, out of TH2's scope."* Flux **inherits**
   Plateau's four under the cascade rather than this lane inventing a theme's
   colours. Storing **by token name** is what makes the stop cheap: the day Flux
   gets four values, every stroke ever drawn re-colours, and no data is touched.

### Surfaced seams — disclosed, not silently absorbed
2. **I1's server-side enum validation was NOT built.** `sync.ts:314-317` states a
   no-revalidation law for this whole jsonb column family; enum rejection there
   would contradict it **and** convert a zero-schema wave into a SERVER-BEHAVIOUR
   ship needing its own word. Validation lives at the **read boundary** instead —
   strictly stronger for the property that matters, since an unknown value can
   then never mis-paint a page, from any client, ever.
3. **The brief's I2 mount does not fit the live geometry.** It said `inset:0` on
   the paper column — written from the Journal, whose sheet grows and whose window
   scrolls. Free Write's `.mode-page` is fixed-height with an inner scroller, so a
   canvas there would nail ink to the viewport and let screen two's ink land on
   screen one's. The stratum mounts inside `.mode-scroll`'s content wrapper, and
   **I7's anchor check is re-aimed at that box.**
4. **The framed band has no location line.** CD1 S1 retired the crumb; mockup B
   still shows one. The switch keeps its position **relative to the mode strip**,
   the one landmark the mockup and the live band share. The crumb was not
   resurrected — that would reverse CD1 S1 on a build lane's authority.
5. **Ink is framed-only (≥1100px)**, per the 112-A rider's precedent; below the
   gate the page keeps its pre-item-121 terms exactly. **This is a reach cost, and
   it is Nick's to weigh:** a tablet in portrait below 1100 gets today's page and
   no ink. One more ticket if he wants it, and no line of this design moves.
6. **`ForwardOnlyEditor`'s `insertMarkerRef` prop is now unused** by every host.
   Left standing: deleting an API from a component three other hosts render is
   past what R15 chartered.
7. **Undo is not unified across a typed run and a stroke**, as the brief asked.
   Free Write is FORWARD-ONLY, and `ForwardOnlyEditor`'s undo stack (FX6 S1) is
   gated to the free-edit modes by that law — a typed-run undo here would breach
   forward-only permanence, an older ruling than this wave. The pen gets an undo;
   the typewriter keeps its permanence. **Both halves are asserted** (S8).

### One thing built on Nick's word, asked because the ruling did not name it
8. **The older zone headed "Ink" retires with STYLING.** It was not ink: three
   hardcoded hex values colouring the TYPED TEXT and caret, beside a "nib · fine"
   button that did nothing — and it was live on journal-origin and legacy pages,
   i.e. most of them. R15 names STYLING and not this, so it was put to Nick
   rather than taken; his word (2026-09-07) was to retire it. `PEN_INKS`,
   ModeStage's unframed pen bar and QuickSprint keep the mechanism untouched.

## §6 · TWO DEFECTS THE HARNESS FOUND THAT READING DID NOT

1. **The caret was not actually dormant in INK.** `ForwardOnlyEditor` sets
   `caretColor` as an **inline** style, which beats any selector, so the caret
   went on blinking — the typewriter "put down" everywhere except where the
   writer was looking. Fixed with `!important`, and the reason written at the
   rule; the alternatives were worse (dropping the `penColor` pass would silently
   change Free Write's text colour, which R15 did not ask for).
2. **The first eraser check passed for the wrong reason.** `penStroke`'s `y` is a
   fraction of element HEIGHT; a stored point's `y` is normalized by WIDTH. The
   sampler guessed one from the other and read empty paper. It now reads the
   persisted stroke's own mid-point and denormalizes it the way the renderer
   does — alpha 102 → 0.

## §9 · THE GATE THE HARNESS CANNOT REPLACE

**THE REAL-DEVICE SITTING ON NICK'S TABLET.** Three things are hardware, and are
unasserted deliberately rather than by omission:

1. **A finger not drawing while a stylus is attached.** CDP has no
   stylus-present state, so S5 asserts **the app's own branch** — once a pen has
   been seen this session, a touch makes no stroke — and says so in the check's
   own name. A check that faked a stylus would assert a thing that does not exist.
2. **Palm rejection**, on a real hand resting on real glass.
3. **The OS handwriting path** on OneUI/Chrome — the whole reason I0 slice 2
   exists. S6 asserts the guard's signature and the no-text OUTCOME; only the
   device can prove the recognizer never fired.

A fourth, softer one: **the three tips looking right.** The harness proves they
are distinct in the data and samplable on the canvas. Whether pencil reads as
graphite and marker as a marker is an eye's verdict, not a suite's.
