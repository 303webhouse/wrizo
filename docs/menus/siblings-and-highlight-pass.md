## §1 · THE SIBLING TAB ROW

### SR0 · THE RECONCILIATION THAT CLOSES A LOOP THIS DESK OPENED

**In PW1 this desk found the Plan panel listing `getBinderPages(projectId)` — the boards in the
page's drawer — and called it THE WRONG SUBJECT.** *"Co-location is not connection."* That finding
stands, and it was ratified.

**Nick's ruling now shows what that set was for.** *"Every other board in that Drawer"* is
`getBinderPages(projectId)`, filtered to boards. **The set was never wrong. It answered SIBLINGHOOD
where MEMBERSHIP was asked.** PW1's erratum was a question put to the wrong reader — and now the
reader gets the question it was always answering.

> **MEMBERSHIP — what this board CONTAINS, or is contained by.** (items 125, 128; PW2's zone)
> **SIBLINGHOOD — what this board SITS BESIDE.** (this row)

### SR1 · THE SET

**Siblings = the other boards in this board's drawer ∪ boards sharing an active tag, from anywhere.**
- **The drawer half is a derivation that already exists** — no new relation, zero schema.
- **The tag half reads the ONE vocabulary** (tag controls, TC1) — never a second tag computation.
- **This board is never its own sibling** (self is absent — the one-grammar-for-refusals rule).
- **Condition boards are never siblings** — the canon: *a condition is not a place.* Shelf and Trash
  do not appear in this row, for the same reason they left "Boards connected" (131(a)).

### SR2 · A PRESS TRAVELS — it does not nest

*"When a user clicks on a Board tag, the Board should just change over to that Board."* **One press,
the surface switches, the crumb updates.** This is **travel**, not **containment**: pressing a sibling
never makes it a member of anything. *(Double-click is the canvas's gesture for travelling INTO a
nested board-card; a tab is a door, and a door takes one press.)*

### SR3 · TWO LISTS OF BOARDS ON ONE SURFACE — Fable's warning, answered in three layers

**Fable's lean holds, and this desk agrees: they are different relations, and both earn their
place.** The work is making sure a writer can never read one as the other. **Three layers do it,
and the third is the one that was nearly lost:**

| | membership — PW2's zone | siblinghood — this row |
|---|---|---|
| **PLACE** | **the rail's side panel** (left, the travelling hand's survey) | **attached below the board**, on the surface (Nick: *"below the Board but attached to it"*) |
| **FORM** | a **list of thumbnails** under section heads | a **row of tabs** |
| **WORD** | section head **"Boards"** under the board's name | **must carry its own visible word — see below** |

**⚠ THE WORD IS WHERE THE DEFECT WOULD LIVE, AND PW2's RULING (1) MADE IT SHARPER.** That ruling
removed **"Linked to this board"** as a visible line, correctly — but it was the one visible phrase
that said *membership*. **What remains on the eye is the bare section head "Boards."** A second list
of boards now arrives. **Two lists, and the only word on one of them is the generic noun.**

**RECOMMEND: the sibling row is labelled by what siblinghood IS — its drawer.** The row's lead reads
the drawer's name (**`Novel`**), because that is the true statement: *these sit where this one sits.*
A sibling found **by tag from another drawer** carries its own drawer as a quiet second line, so a
writer can see *why* it is there. **Place and form already separate the two; the word makes the
separation readable rather than merely spatial.**

**Canon check:** effect (1) — *"a surface's own connections shown ON THE SURFACE"* — **is satisfied:**
siblinghood is this board's own connection, shown on the board. **No conflict with "containers on
the rail"**; the rail still carries the membership survey.

### SR4 · THE TAGGED SURFACES "LISTED WITH IT" — ⚠ HIS SENTENCE HAS TWO READINGS

*"…as well as listed along with their other tagged surfaces (Pages, Cards)."*

- **(a)** the **row itself** carries pages and cards alongside the board tabs — a mixed row
- **(b)** under an active tag, the tagged **pages and cards** appear **in a strip attached to the
  row**, while the tabs stay boards

**Lean: (b).** A tab is a **door to a board**; a page or card in the same row would be a tab that
does something else on press (a page travels, a card opens where you are — PW5), which is the
mixed-act row PP4 forbids. **(b) keeps tabs meaning one thing** and still lists the tagged surfaces
*with* the boards, under the thumbnail law (boards wide, pages tall, cards their own proportion).
**SR-Q1 — his word.**

### SR5 · FILTERED BY TAG — ALL, and it states itself (§3 applies in full)

Under active tags the row shows **only boards carrying ALL of them.** The active tags are visible
and **individually droppable**; an **empty result says so in words**.

## §2 · THE TERM HIGHLIGHT

### TH1 · SCOPE — "VIEWABLE TEXT" MEANS WHAT IS RENDERED, NOT A SEARCH

**Recorded as a constraint:** the highlight paints **only text currently rendered on the current
surface.** It is **not** a document-wide search, never scrolls to a match, never counts matches.
*(Find is the search; this is a lens over what is already in front of the writer.)*

| surface | viewable text |
|---|---|
| **page** | the paper's rendered text |
| **opened card** | the card's text |
| **board** | **the text of the cards DISPLAYED on the canvas — and nothing else.** The board has **no text of its own** (item 124). **A member not shown on the canvas is not viewable** (item 125), and **a card hidden by an active filter is not viewable** (item 108). |
| **thumbnails** | **not painted.** A 7px excerpt in brass is noise, not information; a thumbnail's match is already told by its presence in the filtered set. |

### TH2 · MATCHING — recommend case-insensitive, whole word or whole phrase

`stark` paints **Stark**, **STARK**, **stark** — and **not** *starkly*. A multi-word tag paints only
the whole phrase. **All active tags paint at once**, since selection is ALL (§3). *(Recommended, not
ruled: TH-Q2.)*

### TH3 · ⛔ ONE REGISTER — a constraint, not a hint

**The decoration rides the reveal-on-click register and no other.** That register is
**`store/draftDecoration.ts`** — `decorateEditorFor`, `decorateMarkdownForCard`, `revealAtCaret` —
driven by **`selectionchange`**, the only event that fires for every way a caret moves. **The ledger's
own words:** *"page and card TOGETHER: one register, one behaviour. Splitting the surfaces is what
produced the divergence being cleaned up."* **A second decoration engine walking the same
contenteditable is that divergence, rebuilt.**

**⚠ AND THE REGISTER'S OWN RULE 1 CREATES A HAZARD THE HIGHLIGHT MUST NOT IGNORE.** `draftDecoration`
refuses to redecorate under a **non-collapsed selection**, *because redecorating rewrites the DOM* —
and a DOM rewrite under a live selection destroys it. **A term highlight implemented as more wrapper
spans inside that rewrite inherits the hazard**, and every tag press becomes a DOM rewrite of the
writer's text.

**RECOMMEND, HANDED UP (a new mechanism, so it is flagged as one):** **the register COMPUTES the
ranges; the CSS Custom Highlight API PAINTS them** (`CSS.highlights` + `::highlight(tag-term)`).
- **One register still decides what is highlighted and when** — the constraint holds: one signal,
  one computation.
- **Painting touches no DOM**, so it cannot disturb a selection, a caret offset, or the
  marker-reveal the register already performs.
- **It is styled independently of `::selection`** — which is the whole of §2's brass problem (TH4).
- **Not used anywhere in the tree today** — a builder's S0 must confirm support on every shell the
  app ships in before it is relied on. **If it is refused, the highlight lives inside the register's
  rewrite, obeys rule 1, and repaints when the selection collapses.**

### TH4 · THE BRASS FORK — **RULED, REVERSED, THEN REFINED (2026-09-16)** — see `tag-colour-foundation.md` §0

**The chain, verbatim, so a later reader sees a founder's reversal and not a drift:**
1. **OLIVE** — *"Let's make the tags and the tag highlights the olive green color so that orange stays
   connected to the User producing writing or making choices/progress."* **→ superseded.**
2. **REVERSED TO ORANGE** — *"Eh, I've changed my mind. When a user selects a tag, let's just make the tag
   orange and use orange ('brass') as the background highlight for each matching word."*
3. **(B), REFINED** — *"If the user highlights over an already-highlighted word, make the tagged word's
   highlight a few shades lighter than brass, but still orange so the tag is still visible."* **`::selection`
   stays brass; a matching word takes a LIGHTER orange.**

**The colour rule — "olive marks where you are; orange marks what you do" — was put in front of its author at
its first exception, held once, and has now been set aside BY HIM, FOR TAGS.** Where-you-are markers keep
olive.

**⚠ AND THE FORK'S DECIDING CASE IS OPEN.** By the Highlight API spec (§4.2.4), custom highlights paint
**beneath** `::selection`; item 122's selection is opaque, so **a tagged word under a selection is invisible
(measured 1.00:1).** Nick holds Q-OV1 — `tag-colour-foundation.md` §1–§3, `tag-overlap-mock.html`.

*Treatment B's underline, below, did not survive in any version: item 122 made underline a writer's mark.
The fork as it was first put to him is left standing as the record of what he chose between.*

#### *The fork, as first presented:*

**BRASS IS SPOKEN FOR.** Item 122 ships `::selection{ background:var(--brass); color:var(--on-brass); }`
(`index.css`). **Two meanings in one colour on one page is the problem.** The sharpest case: **a
writer selects a highlighted term — and two meanings stack on the same glyphs in the same colour.**

| | **A · BRASS FOR THE TAG** *(Nick's lean)* | **B · THE TAG DISTINGUISHED ANOTHER WAY** |
|---|---|---|
| tag term | **brass** | an **olive underline** (the accent-rest token) |
| selection | **re-treated** — moved off brass | **unchanged** — brass, as item 122 shipped |
| cost | **re-opens item 122's shipped selection ruling** | none to a shipped ruling |
| stacked case | legible — two different colours | legible — underline under a brass fill |

**THE CANON, placed in view rather than used to override his lean.** The Plateau foundation, live in
the stylesheet's own comments: ***"olive marks where you are; orange marks what you do"*** — and,
beside it, *"informational, not evental."* **A tag highlight marks where a term IS** — informational.
**A selection is something the writer DID** — evental. **Read strictly, the house's colour law
assigns the highlight to olive and the selection to brass, which is treatment B.** Treatment A
therefore asks for **an exception to the colour canon**, not only for a re-treated selection.

**This is handed up, not decided.** Nick's lean is A, and he may well want it — brass is warm, and a
highlight that is meant to be *seen* has a claim on the colour that is meant to be seen. **He should
choose it with the canon line in front of him**, because A is the first place that line would bend.
**TH-Q1.**

## §3 · Q-2 CLOSED — ALL, AND TAGS ARE A SORTING MECHANISM

**Nick:** *"All of them. It should be a kind of sorting mechanism."* **His framing governs the
design, not only the predicate:** **each tag added makes the set smaller** — the writer is sorting a
body of work down to what they want.

**Applies EVERYWHERE the filter lands** — board canvas · sibling tab row · thumbnail menus ·
Shelf/Trash views. **This supersedes item 108's T4 ("one tag at a time") and the pass's "one"
default, both struck in place.**

**S1 · AN EMPTY RESULT STATES ITSELF, IN WORDS.** Under intersection, emptying the set is a
**normal outcome**, and an empty row reads as **breakage**. The line names the tags that produced it —
*"Nothing carries all of #stark, #crypt and #lore."* — **so the writer knows it is the sort, not the
app.** *(No count: the no-count law stands; this is not a destructive confirmation.)*

**S2 · EVERY ACTIVE TAG IS VISIBLE AND DROPPABLE ON ITS OWN.** Narrowing to nothing is only
recoverable **if the last step is visible**: each active tag shows as a pressed chip with its own
remove, **and "clear all" is a separate, second act.** *A writer who added four tags and got nothing
should be able to take back the fourth, not start again.*

**S3 · ORDER OF ACTIVE TAGS = ORDER ADDED.** The chip row reads left-to-right as the writer's own
sequence of narrowing, so the last step is always the rightmost — **the one most likely to be taken
back is always in the same place.**
