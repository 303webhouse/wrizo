## §2 · ITEM 130 — THE STRIP IS REACHABLE AGAIN

**The mechanism**, re-derived from source rather than taken on report:
`.desk-frame-strip` is `position:absolute` at
`left:calc(-1 * var(--frame-host-pad-x))`, width 84px.
`.desk-frame-stagecol` is the normal-flow sibling that **follows it in the
DOM** and begins at the host's own padding-left. **Neither carried a
`z-index` anywhere in `index.css`**, so painting order was DOM order and the
later sibling won. `--frame-host-pad-x` is `clamp(16px, 3vw, 40px)`, so at
1280px the stage began at 38.4 and covered the strip's **38.4–84** band —
roughly its right 54%. A strip item's own centre (x≈42) landed inside the
overlap and `elementFromPoint` there returned `.desk-frame-stage`.

**The fix is stacking, and the distinction is load-bearing.** `z-index:1` —
the smallest value that beats `auto`, so the rule claims no more of the stack
than it needs. **Geometry was the wrong lever and is deliberately untouched:
moving the stage clear of the strip would move the PAPER, and paper never
reflows for chrome.** A geometry fix would have traded one law for another.

**The safety argument is a census, not an assumption** (in the CSS beside the
rule): the cascade panel, the tool sliver and the tutor panel/grip are all
absolutely positioned **inside** `.desk-frame-stage` and sit right of the band
— the cascade is anchored flush at the strip's own right edge, x=84 — so
nothing legitimately overlaps the strip and nothing falls under it. Modals,
backdrops and toasts are `fixed` at z-index 80–300, far above.

### Why it survived so long, and what the harness does about it

**Every other harness reaches these controls with `.click()`**, which
dispatches straight at a node it already holds and **never consults the
hit-testing stack**. A control can be completely unreachable by a human and
pass such a check forever. `pw1.mjs` found it only because it drives real
pointer events.

So **`item130.mjs` never clicks anything.** It asks the document what is on
top at the point a finger would actually land, at **1100 · 1280 · 1366 ·
1680** — four widths because `--frame-host-pad-x` is a `clamp`, so the
overlap's width varies with viewport (33px at 1100, 38.4 at 1280, 40 clamped
above); one width would prove one arithmetic case, not the law.

**Two guards keep the green honest:**

- a **CONTROL** asserting the stage still geometrically overlaps the strip —
  if that ever reads 0 the fixture has stopped exercising the condition and
  S1's pass would mean nothing;
- the **0.00 paper-flush** assertion on the same fixture at the same widths,
  so the repair can never buy reachability with the paper's position without
  this file going red.

**Pre-fix, 5 of 16 red** — every strip item at all four widths reporting
`topClass: desk-frame-stage desk-frame-stage--prose`.

## §3 · THE PEN BAR — A DIAGNOSIS CORRECTED, THEN A RENAME

**The desk's first reading was wrong, and the correction is the ticket's real
work.** It read the phone's "INK" bar as the Ink wave's options zone rendering
where the stratum never mounts — a locked door wearing paint. **S0 falsified
that.**

The bar is the **old `PEN_INKS` text-colour bar** (`ModeStage.tsx`), gated
`{!framed &&}` so it renders **only** below the gate — which is exactly why
the narrow shell is the one surface it reaches. Its swatches are the three
TEXT colours. The Ink wave's own zone lives in `Sliver.tsx` behind the framed
path and cannot render there at all, **so G3 already held by construction and
there was nothing to gate.**

**One word misled it, and dating settled it:** `nib · fine` was introduced
`d65065c` on **2026-06-28**, ten weeks *before* the Ink wave's own
`inkNibFine` (`af3c79c`, **2026-09-07**). Same word, unrelated control.

**The rename's own argument, found in the survey:** that bar's `aria-label`
**already read `'Pen'`**. One control carried two names, and assistive tech
had been getting the right one while the eye got the wrong one. The label now
comes from `modeBarPen`, so the eye agrees with the aria-label rather than the
reverse. `spreadLensInk` folds Spread's inline `'Ink'` chip in beside it —
both through the lexicon, both retiring inline literals.

**The one real locked door is removed.** `nib · fine ▾` carried
`title="Nib styles — coming soon"`: a control promising a capability this
surface does not have. It is **absent, never grayed** — what isn't built
doesn't render. **Item 131 (phone ink)** brings real nibs here. Its orphaned
`.mode-nib` rule was swept in the same commit as its markup; the CD1 comment
that *names* `.mode-nib` among the looks the sliver ported is left intact,
because that sentence is history and stays true of what was ported.

**Deliberately unchanged:** the swatches keep `aria-label="Ink <hex>"`. The
bar is the **Pen**; the swatches are its **inks**. Tool and colour, not two
names for one thing.
