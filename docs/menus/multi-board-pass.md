## §3 · ⭐ THE TWO OPTIONS — they differ on ONE load-bearing thing: **WHAT THE TAB BAR MEANS**

### ⬥ OPTION A — **THE ROW IS THE OPEN SET** *(the browser model)*
**The tab bar lists the boards you have OPEN**, in the order you opened them; **the panes on screen are
marked**; closing a tab closes the board. **Related boards are not tabs** — they live in the Plan menu's
Connected Boards and on the canvas as board-cards.
- **The library (All Boards) is where you FIND a board; the row is what you are WORKING with.**
- **Its cost, stated plainly: it supersedes his own earlier words** — *"all connected boards are listed as
  tabs"* (item 144's charter). **Under A, a connected board is not a tab until you open it.**
- **Its gain:** the row is short, means one thing, and **split is legible** — the marked tabs *are* the
  panes.

### ⬥ OPTION B — **THE ROW IS THE RELATED SET, AND SPLIT IS A MARK ON IT** *(144 as designed, extended)*
**The tab bar stays what item 144 designed** — connected, sibling and tagged boards in a stable order —
**and a board that is on the surface wears a SPLIT MARK.** Opening a second board marks a second tab.
- **Its gain: his earlier ruling survives intact**, the order never moves, and the split is a state of
  something already in front of him.
- **Its cost: a board opened from the library that is NOT related has no home in the row.** It appears as
  a **VISITING tab** for as long as it is open — *which is a second meaning inside one row, and the honest
  price of B.*

| | **A — the open set** | **B — the related set** |
|---|---|---|
| what a tab is | a board you have open | a board related to this one |
| split shown by | the tab **is** the pane | a **mark** on the tab |
| his 144 charter | **superseded** | **kept** |
| an unrelated board | a normal tab | a **visiting** tab |
| row length | short (what you opened) | the drawer's related set |
| where "most recent" lives | the library only | the library only |

**THE DESK DOES NOT PICK.** *A is cleaner about the surface; B is truer to what he already ruled. That is
a founder's choice about which of his own two sentences governs.*

## §4 · THE DRAG — and ⚠ one gesture with two possible meanings (MB-Q2)
**Dragging a board's row onto the surface:** **the drop POINT chooses the pane** — *left half → the left
pane, right half → the right pane, the far edge → a new pane* (the snapping grammar every desktop already
teaches).
**⚠ BUT THE SAME DRAG ONTO THE SAME CANVAS ALREADY MEANS SOMETHING ELSE IN 144: nesting** — *put this
board inside the one I am on.* **One gesture, two meanings, and nobody has ruled which the canvas gets.**
- **LEAN: the CANVAS nests; the EDGE splits.** *A drop into the middle of the work is a placement; a drop
  at the edge is a window act.* **The edge lights up as a landing place while a board is dragged**, so the
  two are told apart before the release, not after.
- **RIVAL: the drag always splits**, and nesting stays the "＋ BOARD" door, which is explicit and already
  designed. *Its case: one drag, one meaning; its cost: the most natural way to say "put this inside that"
  stops working.*

## §5 · ⚠ HIS OWN COLLISION, DRAWN RATHER THAN ARGUED (MB-Q3)
**"From most recent to least recent"** is an order **that moves while he works**. **Item 144's row promises
the opposite** — *"move back and forth between with a single click"*, which needs the tab to still be where
his hand left it.
- **Both options resolve it the same way: THE LIBRARY MOVES, THE ROW DOES NOT.** *The list you open to
  search is ordered by what you touched last; the row you work from holds still.*
- **The mock makes it visible:** press **"use a board for a minute"** and the All Boards list jumps to the
  top while the tab row stays put — **then flip the rig's row-order switch to "most recent" and press it
  again, and watch the tabs move under your hand.** *He chooses by seeing a list move, not by reading this
  paragraph.*
