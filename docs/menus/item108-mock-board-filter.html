The second argument is the one this mockup exists to show, and it inverts the obvious: reflow looks friendlier and is the dishonest one. A reflowed board is complete, tidy and smaller — which is what a board looks like after you delete things. The gaps in HOLD are not a cost; they are the sentence “there is more here than you are looking at,” written in the layout itself. That matters because item 125 and T3 both turn on one risk: a filter must never read as removing. And only one of these two options can get it wrong: HOLD moves nothing, so it owes no restore and has no silent failure available to it. Press a tag on either board, then clear.
