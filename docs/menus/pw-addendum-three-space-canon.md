## §1 · THE RE-READ — THE CANON IS A STRONGER ARGUMENT THAN THE ONE THE PASS MADE

**The diagnosis sharpens, and Nick is right that it is the canon's.** The pass said the
Plan panel lists the *wrong set* — `getBinderPages(projectId)`, the drawer's boards,
instead of the page's. Under the canon that is imprecise in a way that matters:
**both are container questions; they have different SUBJECTS.**

> `getBinderPages(projectId)` answers *"which containers live inside this drawer?"*
> The writer asked *"which containers hold this page?"*

**Wrong subject, not merely wrong set.** That is a better sentence than the pass's own,
and it is the one to keep. Proximity is a container relation between *boards and a
drawer*; connection is a container relation between *a page and boards*. The panel was
answering about the neighbours instead of about the thing in the writer's hand.

**The rail is the container space — which settles PW11 from first principles.** The
pass had to argue PW11 (geography lives in the cascade, not the Desk) from R13.iv and
G8. The canon makes it structural. Read the rail as built: **Journal · Page · Plan ·
Drawers · Shelf · Trash** — five of those six are *containers*, and the sixth, `Page`,
opens a panel whose substance (Places: one home, many memberships) is **also containers**.
**The far-left rail simply is the container space of this app.** A list of boards is a
container list; it belongs there and nowhere else. The Desk is the hand that acts on the
surface under you (G8), and other containers are not that.

**Three candidates stop being conventions and become consequences:**

- **PW5** (*a card opens where you are; a page is travelled to*) was inherited from built
  behaviour. The canon **derives** it: a **card is content held by a container**, so it
  can be *displayed* wherever the writer stands; a **page is a surface**, so you go to it.
  The distinction the code already draws turns out to be the canon's, made early.
- **PW14** (*a plan board is born empty*) was argued from A16 and "the hands don't
  duplicate." The canon is blunter: **the pairing is a relation between a surface and a
  container; containment is a different relation.** Auto-containing the pair would
  conflate two spaces.
- **PW12** (*no new name for "the Shelf"*) holds and hardens: Shelf is already a
  container, and a "shelf of boards" would be a container of containers wearing another
  container's name.

**PW13 survives with a better reason.** The pass called the Plan tab *surface-aware*.
Under the canon the truer statement is that **a Board is a container the writer can also
stand inside** — which is precisely why R13.vi's Outline works (*"technically a Board,
dressed as a Page"*: one container, a different **display**). So Plan does not switch on
surface kind; it always answers **the container question for where the writer is
standing** — on a page, *which containers hold this*; on a board, *this container's own
acts*. Same question, asked of the current position.

**And the canon retro-explains a line Pass 4 wrote before the word existed.** BD1's own
experts section: *"The view is not the work"* — controls that change how work is **seen**
without touching what it **is**. That is the **displays** space, found by the Board pass
and named by the canon. The canon is not new furniture; it is the name for a distinction
this house kept discovering.

## §2 · THE ONE CORRECTION THE PASS OWES

Per this house's own discipline the falsified clause is **left standing verbatim in the
merged pass and corrected here, not rewritten in place.**

**SUPERSEDED IN PART — `pw-pass-page-plan-workflow.md` §THE ARCHITECTS, A9:**

> **A9 · Edits "sync both ways" for free, and the pass claims no credit for it.** The
> cluster pass established it: there is **one card**, seen from two places — identity,
> not a sync engine. The rail's card popup is the board's card popup with a different
> backdrop. Nothing to build, nothing to reconcile, nothing to get wrong.

**Still true, and only of what it was written about:** the *same* card seen from the
canvas and from the rail is one row, and those two views cannot drift.

**Falsified as a general claim by "cards are board-owned but copyable across boards."**
A **copy is a second card**. Two copies do **not** sync, must not be implied to, and the
sentence "edits sync both ways" would become a lie the moment transfer ships if it were
allowed to stand unqualified. **Successor: PW16 below.** The distinction is not a defect
in the canon — it is the canon doing its job: *ownership* is a container relation, and
copying makes a new owned thing rather than a second view of an old one.

## §3 · NEW CANDIDATES

**PW15 · THE SUBJECT, NOT THE SET.** The Plan panel's defect is restated as the canon
states it: it answers a container question about the **drawer** when the writer asked one
about the **page**. `planBoardId ∪ getBoardsPinning(entryId)` is not a better set — it is
the set that has the right **subject**. (Supersedes the pass's own phrasing in §0 and A1;
the mechanism is unchanged.)

**PW16 · ONE CARD, TWO VIEWS — SYNCED. TWO CARDS, ONE LINEAGE — NOT SYNCED.** The rail's
popup and the canvas are two **displays** of one card and cannot drift (A9's true half).
A card **copied** to another board is a **new, board-owned card**; it does not sync, and
nothing in the chrome may suggest it does. **Provenance carries instead of state**, on the
built field: `sourceEntryId` / `portedAt` on `Box` — *"provenance travels on every box"*
(FX5 S3), already shipping, already giving ported cards their travel affordance.
**Zero schema.**

**PW17 · THE COPY DECLARES ITS LINEAGE IN PW10's OWN SLOT.** A copied card's second line
names where it came from — *from Winterfell timeline* — in the same one-line slot that
already names a board's relation or drawer. No badge, no colour, no count; one quiet
sentence, and the writer is never surprised that an edit here did not land there.

**PW18 · A DISPLAY OWES AN HONEST ORDER.** The canon makes the rail's card list a
**fourth display** of a board, beside Open · Storyboard · Outline — and a display that
contradicts the others is a bug the pass could not previously name. Today `buildSurvey`
maps `boxes` in **array (creation) order** — a display choice nobody made. Candidate: the
survey lists in the container's **own reading order** (by `y`, then `x` — the arrangement
the writer actually authored), which is the only ordering the container asserts. Costs
one sort over fields every box already has. *(Storyboard/Outline may assert their own
orders; if they do, the display should follow the board's current one — flagged, not
assumed, since displays are the space this desk has least text on.)*

## §4 · CARD TRANSFER, AS A JOURNEY

**The act, in the canon's words:** move content between containers. The writer is on a
page, working; a card on Stark also belongs on the Winterfell timeline.

**The door already exists, and it is the geometry the pass already designed.** The Plan
panel lists the writer's boards; the survey beside it lists one board's cards. **A card in
one column and its candidate containers in the other is a transfer view that needs no new
level** — G4 holds, because the target list is the panel already open, not a third
pop-out.

**The mechanism is built, and it is not a new pattern.** `SurveyItem` already carries
`renderMenu`, and the survey already ships a per-row quiet disclosure for exactly this
class of act: **`BoardRowMenu` — Move (via `AddToSheet`) and Delete** on board rows.
**Card transfer is that same grammar one level down.** This does not breach PP4's
mis-click law: PP4 forbids a competing *primary* act on a row; a quiet disclosure is the
counterpart the house already shipped.

**The verbs teach the kind (CA1), and here the canon does the sorting:**

| card kind | the act | why |
|---|---|---|
| **free / text card** | **Copy to <board>…** | board-owned content; a copy is a new owned card (PW16) |
| **page-pin card** | **no copy verb — it is already Pin** | a page-pin is *membership*, not content. "Copying" it to another board just means pinning the page there — which Journey B does with a checkbox. Nothing to copy. |

**That asymmetry is the teaching**, and it is CA1 exactly: derived cards carry membership
verbs, free cards carry card verbs. It falls straight out of the canon rather than being
imposed on it.

**COPY, not MOVE — deliberately.** Nick's sentence says cards are board-owned but
**copyable**. It does not say movable. A Move verb would **transfer ownership**, which is
a different act in a canon whose whole point is that ownership is a container relation.
**This desk will not infer it. Q12.**

**The journey and its arithmetic** — from Journey A's step 3, the writer already looking
at Stark's cards:

| | presses |
|---|---|
| open the card's quiet disclosure | 1 |
| press **Copy to…** | 2 |
| press **Winterfell timeline** | 3 |
| **and the writer never left the page they were writing** | **back: 0** |

**Three presses, and no return trip is owed** — because under PW5 the writer never
travelled. This is the strongest case yet for the pass's central claim: the cheapest
surface switch is the one that does not happen.

**Rendered:** Journey A gains a step for it (`pw-journey-a-return-trip.html`, step 9),
built on the step-3 screen so the two-column geometry is doing the work in view.
**Alternate hosts named, not chosen:** the opened card's own dock (C3's hands at card
scale — where a writer *reading* a card would reach), and `AddToSheet`'s existing
move-to-container sheet. The inline disclosure is this desk's recommendation because it
keeps the target list beside the card instead of over the page. **Q13.**

## §5 · QUESTIONS — RAISED BY THE CANON, NOT ANSWERED HERE

*(Q1–Q9 stand in the merged pass. These are new.)*

**Q10 · The verbatim text.** Where does item 123 actually live? It is on no ref in either
repo. Until it lands, §1–§4 are re-read against a four-line summary, and this desk has
marked every place that matters.
**Q11 · Is "container" recursive?** May a board be *in* another board? The canon says
Boards are containers and cards are board-owned; it does not say whether containers nest.
PW13's Plan-on-a-board deliberately does **not** list "the containers holding this board"
because the answer is unknown.
**Q12 · COPY only, or COPY and MOVE?** Default **COPY alone**, on Nick's own word. Move
transfers ownership and is a distinct act.
**Q13 · Transfer's host** — the survey's inline per-row disclosure (recommended), the
opened card's dock, or `AddToSheet`'s sheet. They compose; the question is which is
primary.
**Q14 · PW18's ordering** — does the rail's display follow the board's *current* display
(Open/Storyboard/Outline), or always the container's reading order?
**Q15 · Where do the three spaces bind?** This addendum treats the canon as governing
**design language and argument**. If it is also meant to govern **naming in the product**
— if a writer should ever read the words *surface*, *container*, or *display* — that is a
much larger ruling, and it is Nick's, not this desk's.
