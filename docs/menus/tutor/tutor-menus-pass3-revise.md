## FIRST PASS

### THE EXPERTS — the two rosters

**The Interview mounts now.** Revision's first problem is rarely the text — it is the
writer's relationship to the text: what the piece is trying to be, what its author is
avoiding, what the book's own facts demand of it. Every one of those is served by the
tutor *questioning the writer* — the Socratic reversal Pass 1 proved in miniature —
and an interview needs no page bytes at all. The question travels; the Bible travels
lawfully; v3's sentence covers every byte. The Interview is Revise's counsel that can
exist today, and it is not a consolation roster: working writers name the intention
interview as the single most valuable thing a second reader does before touching a
line.

**The Reading waits for its sentence.** "Read it whole — where should revision
start?" is the mode's signature ask, and it cannot be served without sending the
whole page — old text, which v3's enumeration does not name. So the Reading is
designed here in full — asks, wire shape, gate — and mounts only behind a ratified
sentence that covers it. This is the third dependent of the same gate: 83's BD4
already holds board text behind it; this lane's TD4 holds the selection ask behind
it; the Reading now queues beside them. **One sentence unblocks three counsels
across both hands** — named plainly for Nick's post-vacation ordering.

**Diagnosis, never grades — settled.** The question deferred twice is answered where
it was always headed. Behind the gate, whole-scope *diagnostic* asks are lawful —
where to start, where it drifts, a map of the work. Grade-shapes are absent forever,
in every mode: "is it good," scores, marks out of ten. The app that retired Done
does not introduce graded writing; a verdict on worth is the critic's instrument,
and the Tutor is not the critic even in judgment's own room. TD1 remains the
constitution: locate, diagnose, direct — never supply, and never sentence.

### THE OPPOSITION — named chair: Marketing / simplicity

**Objection 1 — the Interview is therapy theater.** "Ask me where I'm avoiding
looking" reads like a counselor's script, not a craft tool. **Answer:** the bench
below testifies otherwise — avoidance is revision's central mechanic, and the ask
is about the *text's* unexamined places, not the writer's soul. **Conceded in
part:** the phrasing walks a line; the mockup look decides whether it reads as
craft or couch, and the roster drops to two cleanly if it fails.

**Objection 2 — shipping Revise's menu half-gated ships disappointment.** A writer
opens Revise's counsel and finds interviews where readings should be. **Answer:**
the alternative is worse in both directions — mounting the Reading under v3 spends
the disclosure's honesty; mounting nothing leaves judgment's room bare when lawful
counsel exists. G3 keeps the gated rows absent-not-teasing until the sentence
lands, so what ships is whole on its own terms. The gap is named on the lock
sheet, priced, and pointed at the v4 pass.

**Objection 3 — full-wake lenses make Revise the loudest panel in the app.** At
the ceiling by default, in the mode that most needs a clear head. **Answer:** the
mode's desk hand is ground — the Counsel hand carrying more here is the two-hands
design working, not failing. **Conceded structurally:** the at-ceiling default is
flagged, the one-notch alternative stands beside it, and the mockup pair will show
both — the choice is Nick's at the lock, with his eyes.

### THE WORKING-WRITERS BENCH

The testimony, in its own shape: what a reviser wants from a second reader is a
*map before the marks* — where to start, what the piece is for, what to protect.
The intention interview is that map's first draft, and it works before any reading.
The bench's warning completes the arc's set: in Free Write the critic ends
sessions; in Draft, verdicts kill drafts; in Revise, a *grade* ends revision — the
writer who hears "7/10" stops revising and starts defending. Diagnosis keeps the
pen moving; grades hand it to the judge.
