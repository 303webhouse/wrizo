## §0 · THE CONTEXT — a different register, not just different contents

Every pass before this one asked the Tutor about **craft**: energy, thread, drag,
load-bearing, drift. Craft questions have no right answer, which is exactly why
`locate–diagnose–direct` was a comfortable frame — a diagnosis that names a rhythm
problem cannot accidentally become the fix, because there is no single fix.

Nick's order moves Revise onto **correctness**: grammar, usage, paragraph
construction, citation form. Correctness questions are rule-governed and
often single-answered. **The never-supply law has never been tested against a
question that has one right answer.** That is the whole subject of this pass, and
the committee takes it head-on rather than around.

## FIRST PASS

### THE EXPERTS — what a grammar tutor does when it cannot supply the fix

This is the question the order asks, and it has a clean answer in three moves.

**1 · LOCATE BY QUOTATION.** The tutor quotes the writer's own sentence back. The
SYSTEM_PROMPT already makes this lawful in terms: *"Reference atoms are lawful."*
Quoting the writer to themselves is not composition — it is citation of text that is
already theirs. This is how a tutor points when it has no pen.

**2 · NAME THE RULE.** *Subject–verb disagreement. Comma splice. Dangling modifier.
A paragraph carrying two arguments.* A rule is general knowledge, not the writer's
prose; naming one is teaching in its oldest form. The writer who learns "comma splice"
owns it for every sentence they will ever write.

**3 · DIRECT THE CLASS OF FIX, NEVER THE FIX.** *"The subject is plural and the verb
is singular — one of them has to move."* That is a direction. *"Change 'the group of
writers were' to 'was'"* is a correction, and it is barred.

**THE TEST, stated so a build can apply it:**

> **If the tutor's output could be pasted into the page and improve it, it has
> composed. If it can only be understood, and then acted on by the writer's own
> hand, it has taught.**

A correction is *text*. A diagnosis is a *description*. The wall is not about
usefulness or length; it is about whether the reply is transplantable.

### THE HARD CASE, conceded before it is answered

For a large class of mechanical errors **there is exactly one correct fix, and naming
the rule precisely enough to be useful is functionally equivalent to supplying it.**
*"Line four uses 'their' where the possessive isn't meant"* leaves the writer nothing
to decide. The committee will not pretend this is anything other than what it is: **at
the mechanical end, teaching and telling converge.**

**The pass rules it lawful anyway, on a narrower ground than "it's only guidance":**
the never-supply law protects **the writer's authorship of their own bytes** — not
their ignorance of English. The tutor that names the error has taught a rule the
writer now carries; **the keystroke is still theirs to make.** What stays barred, at
every level of obviousness, is **the tutor producing corrected text to be taken.**

This is the narrowest the wall gets anywhere in the arc, and it is named here so that
no future pass discovers it by accident and treats it as a loophole.

### WHY THIS DIFFERS FROM DRAFT — the answer to Nick's actual requirement

Draft asks *what the writing is doing*; Revise asks *whether it is correct*. Craft
versus rule. Draft's verbs are **diagnose and direct**; Revise adds **identify** — a
verb Draft never needed, because craft has nothing to identify against. The rosters do
not overlap at a single ask, which is the difference Nick required.

### THE ARCHITECTS — the mapping

**The mechanics roster reads the page, all of it, always.** Grammar cannot be checked
without text. So **every mechanics ask is a page-reader**, and under the button law
each must name its reading on its own face. They mount with TR3's Reading, behind the
same ratified sentence — candidate B, now in hand — and never before it.

**THE ROSTER — four asks, each naming what it sends:**

- **"Read it whole — where does the grammar slip?"** (grammar and usage)
- **"Read it whole — where do the paragraphs lose their shape?"** (paragraph
  construction)
- **"Read this stretch — what's off in it?"** (the selection row, mechanics-scoped)
- **"Read it whole — where does the citation form break?"** *(mounts only when
  relevant — see below)*

The uniform `Read it whole —` prefix is deliberate: it is the button law's naming made
visible and identical across the row, so consent reads the same way every time rather
than being re-derived from each chip's wording.

**"WHEN RELEVANT" IS DETERMINABLE — it does not need guessing.** Nick's parenthetical
has a real anchor on disk: `decks/library/thesis.ts` deals a **citation ledger** card
among its foundations, and branches humanities/sciences (IMRaD). **The app already
knows which projects have citations**, from the project's own deck. The citation ask
therefore mounts for projects whose deck carries a citation ledger and is **absent
otherwise — absent, not disabled** (G3). No new store, no inference from the prose.

**THE TUTOR NEVER MARKS THE PAGE — two independent bars, both on disk.** The obvious
way to "point" at an error is to mark it in the text. That is closed twice over:

1. **A13** — the component receives no editor reference and no setter. There is no
   road from the panel to the page.
2. **AB2** — marks do not exist as a data structure. The convention set is *"frozen
   tiny"* (bold, italic, headings, spacing), and AB2 names the exact upgrade this
   would require: *"if Draft ever outgrows conventions (Revise-era marks, comments,
   tracked changes), the lawful exit is a structured jsonb column… Nothing in this
   ticket may foreclose that path."*

So **pointing happens in the panel, by quotation** — the tutor says the words back and
locates them; it never annotates. Named for the build brief so no ticket reaches for a
mark that would need a schema this arc has no authority to add.

**LENS POSTURE — question (c), folded and answered.** TR5 argued full wake on the
ground that Revise's desk hand is empty, so the Counsel hand should carry more. **That
argument is spent:** the Counsel hand now carries a whole mechanics roster, and the
panel is no longer bare. Full-wake lenses *on top of* the new roster breaks the bench's
three-section ceiling outright. **The re-pass supersedes TR5 toward rest:** Consistency
and Structure default-collapsed in Revise, headings engraved, memory overriding after
first visit — the same posture Nick's line-2 word gave Draft. The panel's density now
lives in its asks, where the writer summons it.

### THE OPPOSITION — named chair: Marketing / simplicity

**Objection 1 — this is a grammar checker that refuses to work.** The market has fifty
of these and every one inserts the fix; a tool that names the error and declines to
correct it is strictly worse at the job. **Answer:** it is a different job. The
competitors produce a corrected document; this produces a writer who stops making the
error. **But the objection lands where it hurts:** the refusal has to *teach* or it
reads as withholding, and the difference is entirely in the copy. **O-TD1 is
re-flagged here as load-bearing** — the decline's standard is now a shipping
requirement of the mechanics roster, not a nicety.

**Objection 2 — the wall is thinnest exactly here, and the pass admits it.** If
naming the error is the fix, the law has been kept in letter and spent in spirit.
**Answer:** conceded, and answered on the narrow ground above — the writer still
types it. **Named as the arc's thinnest point** so it is argued, not discovered.

**Objection 3 — Revise becomes the densest panel in the app**, after three passes of
arguing restraint. **Answer:** the mode's desk hand is ground by AB2's own law; the
Counsel hand carrying Revise's whole toolkit is the two-hands design working. And the
density is paid for: **the lenses move to rest** to make room.

### THE WORKING-WRITERS BENCH — testifying against the easy answer

The honest testimony first: **most writers reach for a grammar tool to be finished,
not to learn.** A bench that claimed otherwise would be inventing its own witnesses.
The population this roster serves is narrower — the writer who keeps making the same
error and wants it to stop — and it is precisely the population the whole product is
for. **The bench's finding:** a rule named the third time it appears is a rule that
stops appearing, and no tool that silently fixes an error has ever taught anybody
anything.

The bench's warning completes the arc's set: in Free Write the critic ends sessions;
in Draft, verdicts kill drafts; in Revise, **an error inventory ends revision** — the
writer handed forty problems closes the document.

### THE COGNITION / ADHD BENCH — the cap that matters most here

Mechanics is the one subject that can return an unbounded list, and a numbered
inventory of forty errors is the most overwhelming thing this panel could ever
render. **One class at a time.** An ask returns the *most consequential class* of
error with its rule named and a few located instances — never an exhaustive audit,
never a count, never a score (TR4 stands; a count is a grade wearing arithmetic).
Chips render with the panel, fixed order, instant staging, no motion.

**TRR10 · THE DECLINE STANDARD — NO REFUSAL SENTENCE, ONLY THE RETURNING QUESTION.**
*(O-TD1, RULED 2026-08-24.)* Nick's words, verbatim:

> "The Tutor should never say 'No' or 'I can't do that' or 'That request is against my ruleset,' etc. Instead, the Tutor should redirect the User to ask a question that's within the scope of what the Tutor can do, and Socratic questioning should be the preferred way of responding to inappropriate requests (when possible). For example, if a user says, 'What should Character A respond with here,' the Tutor should ask a question back like, 'What does Character A want in this scene, and what is preventing her from getting it?'"

**Elevated (Fable's ruling) to a standing constraint** — binding on **every Tutor
decline everywhere**, not only this roster, and standing beside the voice law and the
button law.

**THE SEAM WITH THE SHIPPED PROMPT, named.** `tutor-rules.md:25` currently reads:
*"decline warmly and briefly, in character, then ask a question that sends the writer
back to their own page… just decline and redirect with a question."* **The shipped rule
permits a decline sentence; Nick's ruling removes it.** Not a restatement — a
narrowing. The amendment is drafted at §2 below and travels to Nick with the roster.

**THE HARNESS PRECISION — a false-positive hazard, named before the ticket.** The
assertion is testable, but a naive refusal-word ban **will fire on lawful tutoring**:
*"the reader can't tell who is speaking"* is good craft counsel; *"this paragraph
cannot carry both arguments"* is a diagnosis. **The assertion must target the Tutor's
first-person refusals about itself** — reply-initial *No*, *I can't / I cannot*, *I'm
not able*, *I'm unable*, *against my*, *my rules / guidelines / ruleset*, *I'm designed
to* — never the modal verbs in isolation.

**THE THIRD REGISTER — a structural note for the record.** The arc now legislates in
three: **ask language** (the voice law, on chips), **button labels** (the button law,
on consent), and now **reply language** (this standard). They point the same way — a
refusal is inherently first-person about the Tutor itself, which is exactly what the
voice law's *at the work, never at the writer* was already reaching for.

**TRR11 · THE STYLE GUIDE CONTROL — MLA defaulted, visibly.** *(RULED 2026-08-24.)*
Nick's words, verbatim:

> "MLA should be the default unless otherwise specified. But we should list Chicago, APA, and AP as options while making it clear that MLA has been defaulted to. These should be clickable buttons under a 'STYLE GUIDE' dropdown toggle in the Revision menu."

**The design.** A labeled **STYLE GUIDE** dropdown toggle in the Revise menu; four
clickable buttons — **MLA · Chicago · APA · AP**; **MLA pre-selected and visibly named
as the default**; the choice **stored per project, never inferred**.

**Under the arc's laws.** The dropdown is a **control, not an ask**: pressing it sends
nothing and sets a stored preference, so the button law does not bind it — nothing
leaves the desk. But the active guide **does** bear on the citation ask, which checks
form against a standard. So the guide **appears on the citation ask's own button**:

> **"Read it whole — where does the citation form break from MLA?"**

— becoming *"…from Chicago?"* when the writer switches. This satisfies the button law
completely (the button names both what it reads **and** the standard it reads against)
and delivers Nick's requirement at the point of use: the default is visible where the
writer presses, not only inside the dropdown.

**Relevance gate — surfaced, not decided.** TRR4 mounts the citation ask only for
projects whose deck carries a citation ledger. **Recommendation:** the dropdown rides
the same gate — both present or both absent, since a style guide on a novel is
furniture with no use. **Alternative, named for Nick:** the dropdown stands in Revise
for every project regardless of kind.

**OPEN — carried, not answered:** **O1** (the chord) — still open, still not invented

## §2 · THE PROMPT AMENDMENT — drafted, travels to Nick with the roster

**The shipped rule, verbatim** (`docs/wrizo-alpha/tutor-rules.md:25` @ `origin/main`):

> If asked to write any part of the work, decline warmly and briefly, in character, then ask a question that sends the writer back to their own page. Never apologize at length; never explain the policy — just decline and redirect with a question.

**What Nick's ruling changes:** the shipped line licenses **a decline sentence and then
a question.** Nick removes the decline. **Only the question survives.**

**PROPOSED REPLACEMENT — for Nick's word, not applied here:**

> If asked to write any part of the work, do not refuse in words. Answer with a question that opens the problem the writer was trying to solve and sends them back to their own page. Never say no; never say what you cannot do; never name a rule, a limit, or a policy; never apologize. The question is the whole reply. Asked "What should Character A respond with here?", answer: "What does Character A want in this scene, and what is preventing her from getting it?"

**Three notes on the draft.**

1. **It keeps the old rule's job and removes only its licence to decline first.** The
   redirect-with-a-question behaviour is unchanged; what disappears is permission to
   narrate the refusal before it.
2. **It carries Nick's worked example into the prompt itself.** An abstract ban on
   refusal language leaks under pressure; a worked example is what makes the behaviour
   reproducible at generation time. His example is used verbatim.
3. **"Do not refuse in words" is deliberate, and the distinction is load-bearing.** The
   Tutor still does not write the prose — the never-supply law is untouched. What
   changes is that **the refusal is never spoken.** The conduct stays; only its
   narration disappears. A build that read this as "the Tutor may now compose" would
   have inverted it.

**This amendment is drafted, not applied.** `tutor-rules.md` is untouched by this pass;
the file changes only on Nick's word, in its own commit, with the shipped version
standing verbatim beneath per the house's supersession practice.
