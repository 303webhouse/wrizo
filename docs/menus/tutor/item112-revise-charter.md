## §0 · WHAT THIS SURFACE IS

Revise is **vocabulary without liveness** — `ModeStrip` carries `live:false`,
`aria-disabled`, and a coming-soon flash. Everything else about it already exists on
paper: the Desk hand ruled (RV1–RV6), the Counsel hand ruled (TR1–TR7 as re-passed), the
error lens fully argued and parked (T1–T7). **This charter builds no new argument. It
builds the floor all of that stands on**, and names what must be true before any tenant
moves in.

The one sentence that organizes the surface, from 83's own pass: **Free Write produces,
Draft marks, Revise dresses.** Everything admitted below must be a form of dressing —
type, correction, counsel about the made thing. Nothing that composes.

## FIRST PASS

### THE EXPERTS — what a surface owes before it opens

**Presence is not composition, and it governs from day one.** A mode that flashes
coming-soon owes nothing; a mode that opens owes a rendered-geometry floor, at both
reference widths, from its first commit. The failure this arc already paid for is
designing tenants for a room with no floor — the census that read mode vocabulary and
never mode liveness. **The charter's first duty is that the floor exists before the
lens, the roster, or the Type section is wired to it.**

**The doorway is the mode's only new gesture.** Revise needs no new way in: it is a
mode on the existing strip, and standing it up means `live:true`, an active state, and
the strip's existing switch behavior. What it must not inherit silently is Draft's
posture — the surface differs in what it *permits*, not in how it is entered.

**Free editing, and the forward-only law's boundary.** Free Write's discipline is
forward-only; Draft permits line rewrites; **Revise is where the writer works
backwards through finished text**, which is the whole reason the error lens is lawful
here and nowhere else. The charter must state plainly that Revise's editor is the
free-editing surface, and that the forward-only instrument does not mount in it.

### THE ARCHITECTS — the mapping

**THE TWO HANDS, MIRRORED (item 119, binding).** Nick's ruling: *"I want the tools menu
pop-out and the Tutor interface pop-out to be exactly mirrored to each other and both
anchored to any Page, Board, or Card on which the User does any kind of writing."*
Revise is a writing surface, so **it carries both hands from day one, not as a later
fitting.** Its Desk hand is 83's Type section (RV1); its Counsel hand is the Tutor panel
carrying the Revise roster. Both anchor to **the writing surface — the paper — never the
screen.** Item 119 supersedes FX18 S2 regime (3) for the Counsel, and this surface must
be built against 119's geometry, not against FX18's superseded clause.

**Coexistence and the announce invariant come along.** Both-open only when the surface
has room; graceful yield. And the errata's hard-won invariant: **drawers announce from
an effect keyed on their own open state**, so every path announces — including paths not
yet written. Revise's hands must be wired that way at birth rather than repaired into it.

**THE FURNITURE — what "full Revise" actually means.** Three tenants, in dependency
order, none of them re-argued here:
1. **The Type section (RV1–RV4)** — face and size, **page-level forever**; alignment and
   indentation absent, not grayed (G3); the paper is the preview, changes apply live and
   revert as cheaply; the sought door for custom fonts renders no row (RV3, shape still
   Nick's).
2. **The Counsel roster** — the Revise asks as re-passed, under the voice law
   (impersonal) and the button law (per-press consent), with the gated Reading behind
   disclosure v4 candidate B.
3. **The error lens (T1–T7)** — parked here, fully argued, in the ruled order
   T1→T3→T2→T4→T5→T6→T7.

**THE ORDER THIS CHARTER PROPOSES.** Floor first, then hands, then tenants:
**112-A the doorway and floor** (`live:true`, free-editing surface, geometry at both
widths, harness) → **112-B both hands, mirrored per 119** → **112-C the Type section** →
**112-D the Counsel roster** → **then the lens's own seven.** The lens is last not
because it matters least but because it is the only tenant that flags the writer's page,
and it should land on a surface that has already been walked.

**DR7 AS NARROWED, restated here because this is the surface it excepts:** *Nothing
arrives unbidden — prose-wide, binding every prose mode, EXCEPT REVISE, by Nick's word.*
The exception is **not a general licence for this surface**: it authorizes the error
lens's flags and nothing else. The Counsel still never solicits; the Type section still
never suggests. **The one thing that may arrive unbidden in Revise is a mark on an
error, because the writer entered the mode to be shown what is wrong.**

### THE OPPOSITION — named chair: Marketing / simplicity

**Objection 1 — full Revise is a large build for a mode most writers may skip.**
**Answer:** the alternative was retired by Nick's word, and for a reason the arc already
demonstrated — the small T0 would have put the error lens on Draft's page, which the
mode boundary forbids and which would have collapsed the law that makes flagging lawful
at all. **Conceded:** the size is real, which is why the proposed order front-loads a
walkable floor rather than shipping all three tenants together.

**Objection 2 — three tenants and two hands make Revise the heaviest room in the app.**
**Answer:** heaviest by count, quietest by default — the Type section is one section, the
roster is a chip row, and the lens is silent until an error exists. **Conceded
structurally:** the lens's default marking density is the real risk and is already
gated by T3's vocabulary landing before T2's marking becomes visible.

**Objection 3 — the surface is being designed by the lane that owns its tenants.**
**Answer:** stated plainly because it is true. This desk holds the Counsel and the lens;
83 holds the Desk hand; 119 holds the geometry. **This charter takes 83's RV1–RV6 and
119's ruling as inputs it may not amend**, and any divergence surfaces to Nick rather
than resolving here. The charter proposes a floor and an order; it does not re-rule
another lane's furniture.

### THE WORKING-WRITERS BENCH

Revision is where writers most want the room to feel *finished* — the draft is done,
the work is dressing it. The bench's warning, consistent with the arc's other two: in
Free Write the critic ends sessions, in Draft verdicts kill drafts, **in Revise a
cluttered room makes the writer stop dressing and start avoiding.** The floor should
open quiet: type controls sought rather than displayed, counsel silent until asked,
flags present but in their own dialect, never Word's squiggles.
