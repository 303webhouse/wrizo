## §0 · HOW THIS PASS TREATS THE COMMITTEES

The report says plainly what it is: *"composite personas… a structured thought experiment,
not user research… read it as a hypothesis to test in a sitting, not a finding."* This
desk takes that at its word and does not treat any committee claim as established.

**The standard applied below.** A claim is *validated* when it matches documented working
practice across more than one well-known process. It is *corrected* when the underlying
need is real but the report states it too strongly or in the wrong shape. A member is
flagged as a **straw figure** when the persona would not be a Wrizo user at all, or when
the persona is named and then never actually walks anything — because a composite that
contributes no steps is coverage on paper and none in fact.

**A note on the report's own accuracy, before its content.** Three of its factual claims
about standing rulings do not survive a check against the ledger, and one of them changes
what two committees are complaining about. They are in **§6**, not buried here, because a
brief that corrects the committees owes the same treatment to the document that convened
them.

## §1 · VALIDATION, COMMITTEE BY COMMITTEE

### Committee One — Fiction

**The Plotter — VALIDATED, with one shape correction.** Beat-sheet-first thriller writing
is real, widely taught and well documented. His walk is the most accurate in the report.

*The correction is at his step 5*, and it matters because it is the report's headline
finding. He asks that *"each scene card has to become a scene or chapter in the
manuscript, in the order the cards are in, still linked to its card."* In practice most
beat-sheet novelists treat the beat sheet as a **reference they write from**, not as a
**generator that manufactures chapters**. The stronger and truer version of the need is
**drift visibility** — *the board and the manuscript disagree, and I can see where* —
which is also what his step 7 actually asks for. Generation is a much larger build than
visibility and buys less. **The convergence stands; the mechanism the report implies does
not follow from it.**

**The Pantser — VALIDATED, and the most valuable member in the report.** The forward-lock
praise rings true and matches the analog law's own reasoning. The 40,000-word "find the
book" moment is a real and well-documented inflection. "Will never tag as they go" is
accurate to how discovery writers actually behave, and the retroactive-tagging need the
report derives from it is correctly derived.

His refusal to choose a type or a structure on day one is the single most useful sentence
in the report, **and it is already Wrizo's own ruling** — item 165's amendment reads: *the
menu's job is getting a writer creating fast, not organizing.* This is convergence with a
standing decision, not a challenge to one, and it should be recorded as support rather
than as a new demand.

**The Worldbuilder — VALIDATED.** The copy-forks-lore warning is the most valuable
technical finding in the report and is correct: a character sheet that exists as two
copies is a character sheet that will disagree with itself, and the failure is silent.

*One sort correction:* the cross-book **timeline** is presented as a near-universal gap.
It is a real need, but working series writers overwhelmingly keep chronology as a page or
a dated list, not as a distinct object. It belongs in a preset, not in the primitives.

**The Screenwriter — VALIDATED, and more specific than the report realises.** Scene
headings as cards with reorder-writes-through is not a wish; it is the working model of
the two tools this member already uses daily, and any screenwriter will expect it. Page
count as the unit of time is likewise literal, not figurative. **This member's needs are
the least negotiable of the twelve**, because they are format law rather than preference.

### Committee Two — Journalists and creative nonfiction

**The Features Writer — VALIDATED, and carries the report's strongest finding.** Quote
provenance — *who said it, when, from which recording, on what terms* — is correctly
identified as the load-bearing object in nonfiction, and the observation that a copied
card keeps *board* provenance rather than *source* provenance is exactly right and exactly
the kind of thing that only shows up in a walkthrough.

**The Beat Reporter — STRAW FIGURE. Flagged.** A daily reporter filing on an hours-long
deadline does not draft in a tool the newsroom does not run. They write in the CMS the
publication mandates, because the piece must land in that system and the desk edits it
there. The report concedes the fatal point itself — *"Export is the product. The editor
doesn't use Wrizo."* A writer whose deadline is measured in hours will not accept an
export step at all; that is precisely the writer who stays in the CMS.

**What flagging him costs, stated so nothing true is lost with him.** Three needs are
attributed to him, and they do not fall together:
- **Story budget** (every piece in flight, status, deadline) — *this is newsroom
  management software, not a writing tool.* It leaves with him.
- **Snippets / boilerplate** — modest, real, and better justified by other writers than
  by him.
- **"Sources outlive stories"** — **this one survives him entirely**, because the Features
  Writer needs it just as much and is credible. It should be re-attributed rather than
  discarded.

**The Memoirist — VALIDATED.** Lifting a *passage* with a link back to the entry it came
from is the truest and most concrete expression of the anchors convergence anywhere in the
report — better than the academics' version, because it names the act rather than the
primitive.

### Committee Three — Academics and researchers

**The Humanities PhD — VALIDATED.** Citations, footnotes, page-numbered quotation and PDF
annotation are the actual daily work of a close-reading dissertation. The judgement that
*"without citation objects, the research board is a pile of PDFs"* is correct and is the
sharpest sentence in the section.

**The Social-Science PhD — VALIDATED IN PART.** Literature review, coding by tag, and
co-authorship are real. **Figures, tables and equations are where this writer leaves** —
they are done in LaTeX or Word, and a half-measure in Wrizo would be used by nobody.

**The Research Professor — STRAW FIGURE BY ABSENCE. Flagged.** He is named in the
membership with several articles, a grant and a book in flight, and then **never walks a
single step**. Section 4.1 walks one dissertation; the only per-member attribution in it
is a parenthetical to the Social-Science member. A persona introduced to widen coverage
and then given nothing to do has widened the *appearance* of coverage only.

**What his absence hides is the interesting part:** the multi-project writer — several
things in flight, at different stages, sharing one source pool — is the one genuinely
distinct academic shape, and it is the shape closest to the Features Writer's and the Beat
Reporter's. **Nobody in the report actually walked it.** If a fourth walk is ever
commissioned, that is the one worth doing.

**One general correction to §4.1.** *"The outline has to be the dissertation's own
structure — its headings ARE its sections"* is stated as a requirement and is true of some
writers and plainly false for many, who keep a prospectus deliberately separate from the
draft. **The conclusion it supports (one spine) survives; the premise as stated does
not** — and the difference matters, because "the outline IS the document" is a much
stronger architectural demand than "the outline and the document must not silently
drift."

## §2 · THE SORT — UNIVERSAL, GENRE, NICHE

**The criterion applied.** *Universal* — a novelist, a reporter and a PhD student all need
it, **and a writer who never opens a preset still needs it**. *Genre* — real and common for
one or two kinds of writing, and expressible as the contents of a preset or a mode's
furniture. *Niche* — real for few, better served outside Wrizo, or carrying a promise the
app should not make.

### UNIVERSAL — the primitives every writer needs

| Need | Why it is universal |
|---|---|
| **Retrieval over organization** | The pantser, the reporter and the Zettelkasten researcher say the same sentence: *don't make me file, let me find.* Already ruled and designed (this desk's FIND pass, FN1–FN8). |
| **Drift visibility between plan and manuscript** | Every committee hit it. Stated as *visibility*, not auto-generation — see §1's Plotter correction. |
| **Reference rather than copy, for anything that is a source of truth** | Lore, quotes, citations. The failure mode is identical in all three: a silent fork. |
| **Versions / snapshots** | Fiction's *"keep the old ending"*, journalism's editor drafts, academia's advisor drafts. Three names for one need, and the report files them separately. |
| **Status on a unit of work** | Scene, piece, chapter. The unit differs; the need does not. |
| **Reversible choices** | Discovery writers exist in all three committees. |
| **Export in the form the reader judges** | Every writer ends outside the app. |

**One promotion the report does not make.** It lists versions three times under three
committees and never as a convergence. **It is one need and it is universal**, and it is
cheaper than most of the genre features above it.

### GENRE — belongs in a preset or a mode's furniture

Story structure and beat sheets · scene-card-to-scene binding · screenplay pagination and
revision colours · **source records with fields** · quote provenance · citations,
footnotes and bibliography · **import from reference managers** (Zotero, BibTeX, RIS —
the highest-leverage academic feature after citations themselves) · reading notes bound
to a source · timelines · the fact-check pass · word-count roll-up.

**Two of these are genre in their content but universal in their machinery** — *source
records* and *quote provenance* are both "a structured record, referenced from many
places." That shared machinery is exactly PLAN DESK's Record primitive, and it is why the
research preset cannot be finished by this desk alone (§5).

### NICHE — leave it

- **The locked/private page.** Real feeling, wrong instrument — and the only item here
  this desk argues against on principle rather than on priority. A lock in a writing app
  implies a **security guarantee the app cannot make** while the text sits in ordinary
  storage and syncs to a server. A writer who trusts it and is wrong is harmed worse than
  one who was never offered it. If privacy is ever taken up, it is an encryption ticket
  with a threat model, not a page setting.
- **Audio import and transcription** — the report already says "large, and later." Agreed.
- **Equations, figures and tables** — where the social scientist leaves for LaTeX.
- **Story budget and embargo dates** — newsroom management; leaves with the straw reporter.
- **LaTeX export** — one committee, and the one most able to route around it.
- **Coloured revision pages** — real industry practice, but it presumes a production
  pipeline Wrizo is nowhere near.

## §3 · THE PRESETS — item 165's *"three each, the desks choose"*

**The charter, verbatim from the ledger:** *Story Structure (Storytelling / Screenwriting,
**three each, the desks choose**)* · *Outline (Traditional, Mind Map, **open to more**)* ·
*Organize Research (Import Sources → a board of the imports as pages; Bibliography greyed
placeholder)*. **And its amendment, which governs every choice below:** *the menu's job is
**getting a writer creating fast, not organizing**.*

**What that amendment demands of a preset.** A preset that produces empty lanes has
produced an organizing chore. **Every preset below arrives with its cards already in it,
each card carrying one line of prompt** — so the writer's first act inside it is writing,
not naming.

### STORY STRUCTURE → STORYTELLING (three)

1. **THREE-ACT.** The common spine, the one every other structure is a refinement of.
   **Day one:** three lanes — Act I, Act II, Act III — carrying five cards in place: *Hook ·
   Inciting Incident · Midpoint · Crisis · Resolution*, each with a one-line prompt.
2. **THE FIFTEEN BEATS.** Dense commercial plotting — the Plotter's actual instrument.
   **Day one:** fifteen named beat cards in order, each with its rough position in the whole.
   **⚠ NAMING FLAG, and it is a legal question rather than a craft one:** the report calls
   this *"a Save-the-Cat preset."* **Save the Cat! is a trademark and its beat sheet is a
   copyrighted framework.** Shipping a menu item under that name is a different kind of
   risk from choosing it. **This desk names the structure generically and recommends it
   ship generically**; the shape is not ownable, the name is. Nick's or counsel's call.
3. **THE HERO'S JOURNEY.** The mythic/quest shape, which serves the Worldbuilder and the
   whole fantasy tradition directly. **Day one:** the twelve stages as cards, in order.

*Why not a fourth for the Pantser:* because a structure chosen before writing is the one
thing a discovery writer will not do. **He is served in Outline instead, by the preset
below** — which is the honest answer rather than a token slot he would never press.

### STORY STRUCTURE → SCREENWRITING (three)

1. **FEATURE — THREE-ACT WITH PAGE GATES.** **Day one:** three lanes carrying the industry
   page targets as card text (Act I to roughly p.25–30; Act II to roughly p.85–90; Act III
   to the end). The page numbers are the point: this member's structure *is* pagination.
2. **THE SEQUENCE METHOD (EIGHT SEQUENCES).** The standard film-school spine. **Day one:**
   eight sequence cards of twelve to fifteen pages each.
3. **TV PILOT — COLD OPEN AND ACTS.** Because the member writes *"features and TV pilots"*
   and act structure for television is genuinely different, not a variation. **Day one:**
   Cold Open, Acts One to Four (five where the format takes it), Tag.

### OUTLINE (Traditional · Mind Map · **and a third, proposed**)

1. **TRADITIONAL.** **Day one:** a numbered hierarchy (1, 1.1, 1.1.2) with three top-level
   headings already present and expandable.
2. **MIND MAP.** **Day one:** one central card with four radiating children.
3. **PROPOSED THIRD — "FROM MY PAGES" (the reverse outline).** Select pages, and get a
   board of their cards in one act. **This desk recommends it in the charter's own "open
   to more" clause**, and recommends it *strongly*, because it is the same act requested
   three separate times in the report and never recognised as one: the Pantser's
   find-the-book at 40,000 words (§2.1.3), the Memoirist's authored order for a collection
   (§3.1.4), and the report's own first un-thought-of item (§2.4). **It is the only preset
   here that serves a discovery writer at all**, and it costs one selection act.

### ORGANIZE RESEARCH (Import Sources · Bibliography greyed)

- **IMPORT SOURCES — day one:** a board whose cards are the imported documents as pages,
  named by filename, in import order, ready to open and read.
- **BIBLIOGRAPHY — and this is the seam.** It is greyed today, and for the academic
  committee it is *the* feature. **It is also the one preset on this list that a board of
  pages cannot deliver**, because a citation is a record with fields, not a page of prose.
  **This desk cannot finish the research preset alone** — it waits on PLAN DESK's Record
  primitive (§6's A). Named here rather than promised.

## §4 · WHAT THIS DESK CAN SETTLE NOW, AND WHAT IT CANNOT

**Settled now, and not waiting on anyone:** the *contents* of every preset above — which
structures, how many, what cards arrive, what each card says. Those are craft calls and
they are made.

**Waiting on PLAN DESK, and honestly so:** whether a preset's board *binds* to the
manuscript, whether a card can *become* a chapter, and whether a citation can exist at
all. Those are the spine, reference and record questions, and a preset that assumed an
answer would be designing another lane's architecture from inside a menu.

## §5 · WHERE THIS DESK AND PLAN DESK MUST TALK

Five seams, in the order they block work:

1. **Organize Research → Bibliography needs the Record primitive (§6-A).** A hard
   dependency, not a coordination nicety: without records the research preset ships as a
   pile of PDFs, which is the academics' own complaint restated.
2. **"From My Pages" needs the spine answer (§6-B).** Does the resulting board *become* the
   order, or *show* it? The preset is the same either way; **what it promises is not.**
3. **Scene-card-to-chapter needs reference-versus-copy (§6-C).** Every fiction preset here
   produces cards. Whether a card can become a chapter, and stay linked, is their call.
4. **Quote provenance and citation need anchors (§6-D).** Both are preset contents whose
   usefulness collapses to nothing at page granularity — the Memoirist's passage-with-a-
   link is the cleanest test case in the report and should be the one the anchor design is
   checked against.
5. **165's amendment should bind both halves.** *"Getting a writer creating fast, not
   organizing"* is a craft ruling with structural consequences: it argues against
   front-loaded choice generally, which is convergence 8 arriving from the other
   direction. **Neither desk should design a first-run flow that contradicts it.**

**And one thing this desk asks PLAN DESK not to wait for:** the preset contents above.
They are inputs to the architecture question, not outputs of it — *what a Story Structure
board contains* is evidence about what a board must be able to hold.
