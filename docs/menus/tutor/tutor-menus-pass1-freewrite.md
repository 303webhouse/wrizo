## §0 · THE CONTEXT

Free Write is the funnel — mode key `journal`, the posture not the place. The left hand
just ruled itself down to instruments and nothing else; the right hand's standing law
here is FW6: nothing arrives unbidden, the panel opens only by the writer's press.

This pass begins at the apparent contradiction and resolves it. A12 names the Tutor as
the one surface the writer visits when they *don't know what they need* — but Free
Write's whole discipline is *don't stop to ask*. The resolution is temporal: **by the
time the panel is open, the writer has already stepped out of the funnel by their own
act.** The menu's work is not to interrupt the pour — FW6 already forbids that — but to
make the surfacing visit short, useful, and spring-loaded back toward the page. Free
Write's contents therefore serve exactly one moment: the writer surfacing, mid-session,
wondering what they even came to the panel for.

## FIRST PASS

### THE EXPERTS — what guidance the funnel can bear

**The staged ask, named.** The mode-menu mechanic this lane proposes is a row that loads
a ready question *into the composer* — editable, cursor placed, sent only by the
writer's own Send. Nothing fires on stage; the wire moment stays exactly where TU2/TU5
ratified it. This is FX15's deck-drawn precedent applied to conversation: drawn by the
writer's act, never dealt on arrival. And it is directionally clean under G8 — a staged
ask addresses the *Tutor*, lives in the Tutor's own furniture, and has no path to the
page (A13's wall is architectural; the component holds no editor reference to misuse).

**Why staged asks exist at all.** A12's writer doesn't know what they need — and the
blocked writer's deepest version of that is not knowing *what to ask*. A menu of askable
shapes is the tutor's craft knowledge surfaced as questions. That is teaching, not
writing: the writer still asks, still edits, still sends, still owns the exchange. The
bench below sharpens this: the chips should be training wheels a writer outgrows —
"where's the energy" asked three times from a chip becomes a question the writer carries
unprompted. That arc, from offered shape to owned habit, is the tutor-not-ghostwriter
thesis in miniature.

**Free Write wants momentum asks, never judgment asks.** Quality talk invites the inner
critic, and the critic is the funnel's predator. The FW roster is forward-facing by
constitution — energy, thread, motion — and verdict shapes ("is this good," "does this
work") are barred from this mode entirely. They are Draft's and Revise's arguments to
make against the ghostwriter wall in their own passes, not contraband to smuggle in
here.

### THE ARCHITECTS — the mapping

**The asks mount in Talk it through.** No separate mode section. Asks are conversation
furniture, so they live where asking lives: a short chip row inside the Conversation
section, above the composer, below the messages. This keeps the census's conversation-
first order intact and honors Pass 0's Objection-2 concession — the grammar binds this
panel's frame, never its dialogue; arranging the dialogue region is this lane's own
ground. Staging is one press (the row does one thing — G-row law met); editing and
sending remain the composer's existing acts.

**The FW roster, three asks:** *"Where's the energy in what I just wrote?"* ·
*"What thread could I pull next?"* · *"Ask me something that keeps me moving."*
The first two ride the existing delta law — new-since-last-read travels on Send,
nothing changes on the wire. The third reverses the table: the tutor questions the
writer, the most Socratic and most funnel-safe shape guidance takes. Three is the
ceiling, constitutional for this mode (bench: a glance, not a read). All three editable
before Send, always; auto-send exists nowhere.

**The lenses rest.** In Free Write, Consistency, Structure, and Fragments
default-collapsed — engraved headings visible (G5's one ember), one press opens,
per-surface memory overrides the default thereafter (the grammar's memory law: mode
sets the first-visit posture; the writer's history wins after). The offline promise
stands untouched — the lenses above still work — collapsed is not absent.

**The cross-mode body is untouched.** Conversation-first order, the Bible and its
writer-authored wall, the disclosure and its versioning, the meter and its honest
fallback, the delta caps and cursor law — all mode-invariant. Free Write changes
contents only, and only inside its own region.

**What is ABSENT here (G3, applied):** any editing-suggestion row (Draft/Revise
arguments, each owing its own pass against the ghostwriter wall); any pace, streak,
count, or completion content (M1/CD4 — the meter stays the only number, and it is a
cost, not a score); any reference to the left hand's instruments (the cluster is the
Desk's; the hands don't duplicate — G8's spirit applied to contents). Absent, not
disabled, not explained.

### THE OPPOSITION — named chair: Marketing / simplicity

**Objection 1 — a phrasebook Tutor.** Canned questions make the exchange feel scripted;
the writer's own asking is the relationship. **Answer:** three chips, editable, with
the composer primary — the phrasebook risk is real at scale, which is why the ceiling
is constitutional and the rows stage rather than send. **Conceded honestly:** if the
sitting shows the chips reading as buttons-for-content rather than questions-to-own,
the FW roster shrinks to zero cleanly — TFW1 makes zero a lockable outcome, and the
mechanic survives for Draft where density is native.

**Objection 2 — staging is the slope's first step.** Text the writer didn't type,
one press from sending: the machine is nearly speaking for them. **Answer:** the slope
has a floor and it is architectural — staged text addresses the Tutor, never the
reader; A13 gives it no path to the page; the disclosure law's "nothing is ever sent
unless you ask" is untouched because Send remains the only wire act. The optics are a
build requirement, named now for the post-vacation ticket: staged text arrives visibly
editable, cursor in the text, never highlighted as final.

**Objection 3 — Free Write needs no menu at all.** FW6 already gives silence; adding
anything betrays the barest desk. **Answer:** closed cost stays one grip (G7) — the
writer who never opens never sees. A12 is precisely about the writer who does open.
**Partially conceded:** this mode's roster is the smallest of the three passes by
design, and the zero-roster lock is Nick's to choose at the sitting.

### THE WORKING-WRITERS BENCH (this lane's seeded bench)

The testimony shape is consistent: mid-pour, writers want nothing; surfacing, they want
orientation. The question most writers actually carry out of a freewrite — *"is there
anything here?"* — is a verdict shape, and the bench rules it stays out of this mode:
answering it invites the critic into the funnel, and the critic ends sessions. Energy,
thread, and Socratic shapes orient without judging. The bench also names the outgrowing
arc as the roster's success condition: a chip that teaches its question into the
writer's own mouth has finished its work.

### THE COGNITION / ADHD BENCH (inherited, strictest here)

Zero unbidden events — no arrival motion beyond the one engine, no counsel movement
during typing, chips render with the panel and never animate in. Three chips, short
labels, fixed order — no reshuffling between visits (re-scanning is the tax). Staging
is instant: no typewriter-effect theater in the composer fill.

## SECOND PASS — CANDIDATES

**TFW1 · SILENT UNTIL ASKED, THEN BRIEF.** The Tutor's Free Write contents serve the
surfacing moment only; this mode's roster is the smallest of the three passes, and
**zero is a lockable roster** at Nick's word. (FW6's frame; A12; G7.)

**TFW2 · THE STAGED ASK.** A menu row loads an editable question into the composer;
only the writer's Send fires the wire. Staging is one press, addresses the Tutor only,
and has no path to the page. Auto-send exists nowhere. (FX15's deck-drawn precedent;
A13/G8; TU2/TU5 wire law untouched.)

**TFW3 · THE FW ROSTER, THREE ASKS.** *"Where's the energy in what I just wrote?"* ·
*"What thread could I pull next?"* · *"Ask me something that keeps me moving."*
Mounted as a chip row in Talk it through, above the composer; three is this mode's
constitutional ceiling. (Experts; bench.)

**TFW4 · NO VERDICT SHAPES IN THE FUNNEL.** Free Write asks never take judgment forms;
quality talk is Draft's and Revise's argument to make in their own passes, against the
ghostwriter wall, behind the sitting. (Working-writers bench; the anti-revision-
paralysis thesis.)

**TFW5 · LENSES REST IN FREE WRITE.** Consistency, Structure, Fragments default-
collapsed in this mode — headings engraved (G5), one press opens, per-surface memory
overrides thereafter. The offline promise stands: the lenses above still work.
(Bench ceiling; the grammar's memory law.)

**TFW6 · THE CROSS-MODE BODY IS UNTOUCHED.** Conversation-first order, the Bible, the
disclosure, the meter, the delta mechanics — mode-invariant, all of them. Free Write
varies contents only, inside its own region only. (Pass 0 Objection-2 concession;
TU2/TU5.)

**OPEN — carried, not answered:** **O1** (Pass 0's) — the right hand's keyboard chord;
still Nick's or a later pass's, not invented here. **O-TFW1** — whether the chip row is
the shared mount for Draft's and Revise's rosters too; answered in Pass 2, where
density will test it.

**FOR THE SITTING** (checkpoints the log can answer; the lock waits on it either way):
do the chips read as questions-to-own or as buttons-for-content? Is three right for the
barest mode, or is the true Free Write number zero? Do the resting lenses make the
panel feel cleared — or gutted?
