## §3 · THE CONCESSION — the mockup corrects one of this desk's own rules

**This desk's §4 rule 1 was: NO NEW SECTION.** Every automation finding was to land in an
existing lens, and *"if a finding has no lens that fits, argue the lens, do not add a
bay."*

**The mockup adds a `Checks` section, and it is right to.** The rule was written to prevent

section with a clear role, and it is a far better home than the rule's own consequence
would have been: my rule would have forced claim-checking into **Consistency**, which is
the lens for *"these two things disagree"* — a poor fit for *"this has no source."*

**The rule is amended, and this desk states the correction rather than quietly adopting
the mockup's shape:**

The ceiling logic survives intact — what it was guarding against was proliferation, and
one section is not proliferation.

## §4 · A NEW FINDING — the mockup's placeholder breaks FN5's contrast

**The composer's placeholder in the mockup is `Ask about this page…`.** The shipped
lexicon's is `'Ask a question…'`.

**FN5 was built on the contrast between those two registers.** Its whole argument: the
composer's placeholder is *an invitation to speak*, so Find's must **name a scope, not a
question** — recommended `Find in this project…` — *"The two must not rhyme."*

**`Ask about this page…` names a scope.** So under the mockup's wording, **both boxes name
scopes**, and the contrast FN5 depends on is gone — the two inputs rhyme again, which is
precisely the confusion FN3 and FN5 exist to prevent. And the confusion is the dangerous
direction: one box sends the writer's words to a model, the other touches only their disk.

**Three ways out, none chosen here:**
1. **Keep `Ask a question…`** for the composer and the mockup's phrasing is a mockup
   convenience. *Simplest, and preserves FN5 as ratified.*
2. **Take `Ask about this page…`** and re-cut Find's placeholder so the contrast lives
   somewhere else — e.g. naming the *act* rather than the scope, `Search what you've
   written…`.
3. **Let FN3 carry the whole load alone** — the material difference, Figtree against
   Crimson Pro. *Weakest: it puts the entire safeguard on one typographic cue.*

**This desk's lean is (1)**, because FN5 is already ratified and the mockup's phrasing
buys little. **Surfaced, not chosen** — the placeholder is a lexicon string and the
contrast is a ratified candidate, so it is Nick's or Fable's.
