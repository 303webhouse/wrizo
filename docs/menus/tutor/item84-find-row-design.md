## §0 · THE PROBLEM, NAMED PRECISELY

Two text inputs will now sit within an inch of each other in one panel. **One sends the
writer's words to a language model. One touches nothing but the writer's own disk.** The
whole arc's disclosure discipline — candidate B, the button law, the meter, the
disclosure modal — rests on the writer always knowing which is which. Stack two boxes
without designing the difference and the panel quietly becomes the one thing this arc has
spent its life preventing: **a surface where the writer is unsure what left the desk.**

So the design question is not "where does the Find box go." It is **what makes the
difference legible without reading** — and, harder, what makes it legible *after* the
press, when results are on screen and the writer has forgotten which box they typed in.

---

## FIRST PASS

### THE EXPERTS — the difference must be carried by kind, not by label

**Labels are the weakest available signal.** Two boxes distinguished only by button text
("Send" / "Find") are two boxes that look alike, and a writer moving fast reads position,
not words. The difference must be carried by **material** — face, weight, region, and
what happens to results — so that the two are different *kinds of thing* on sight.

**The seam already exists in the app's own vocabulary.** Plateau's law: **Figtree is the
room's hand, Crimson Pro is the writer's voice.** The composer is set in the writer's
voice because what the writer types there *is their voice, travelling*. **A Find query is
not the writer's voice — it is an instrument setting**, the same kind of thing as a lens
or a page control. **Find is set in the room's hand.** That single choice does most of the
work: the two inputs stop looking like siblings and start looking like a sentence and a
dial. It also inverts, correctly, the chip decision — chips are Crimson because a chip
*becomes* the writer's words; a Find query never does.

**The deepest confusion risk is not the input. It is the results.** If Find's hits render
in the conversation stream, they will read as Tutor replies — the panel's most
model-shaped region, where every prior line came from a model call. **Find's results must
never enter the message stream.** They belong to their own region, in the room's hand,
looking like a lens's list, because that is what they are: a local index, presented.

### THE ARCHITECTS — the mapping

**Placement, per Nick's word:** the Find row sits **directly beneath the composer**, inside
`Talk it through`, separated from it by the panel's own 1px rule. Below Find sit the
lenses, unchanged. The rule is not decoration: it is the boundary between *what leaves*
and *what does not*, and it is the only structural line inside that section.

**The row's parts:** a single-line input in the room's hand at the lens type size, its
placeholder naming the instrument's reach rather than inviting a question; and one button
reading **`Find`**. Olive at rest, brass on hover, ember on press, as everything else.

**Placeholder discipline.** The composer's placeholder is `Ask a question…` — an
invitation to speak. Find's must not be an invitation to speak. It should name a *scope*,
not a *question*: something of the shape `Find in this project…`. **Recommended, for
Nick's word.** What it must never be is a question-shaped prompt, which would make the
two boxes rhyme exactly where they must not.

**THE ENTER-KEY DEFECT, named before it exists.** Two inputs in one section, one keyboard.
If focus is in Find and `Enter` sends the composer — or worse, if `Enter` in either box
reaches the other's handler — the writer fires a model call while looking at a local
search. **Enter in Find performs a Find, and nothing else, ever.** This is a harness
obligation, not a hope.

**THE AFTER-THE-PRESS PROBLEM.** Results render in a **Find results region** directly
under the row: the room's hand, a list of surfaces, each a button that **navigates** —
exactly the Fragments lens's proven mechanic, which travels and never inserts. Results
carry a plain count line. **They never render as messages, never carry a speaker, never
scroll into the conversation.**

**THE METER'S SILENCE, made into a signal.** Every completed ask puts a metered line at
the panel's foot. A Find never will, because a Find costs nothing. Absence is a true
signal but a weak one — a writer does not notice what did not appear. **The desk does not
propose adding a "this cost nothing" line** (it would be chrome about chrome, and the arc
has resisted worse). It proposes instead that the results region's own heading carry the
instrument's nature plainly enough that the meter's absence is confirmatory rather than
load-bearing.

### THE OPPOSITION — named chair: Marketing / simplicity

**Objection 1 — two boxes in one panel is one box too many.** The Counsel's house was
conversation; adding a search bar makes it a toolbar. **Answer:** Nick placed it here by
ruling, and the placement has a real logic — Find and Ask are the two ways a writer asks
*"where is the thing I half-remember,"* and separating them across surfaces would make the
writer choose a room before they know which question they have. **Conceded:** the panel is
now denser, and the density is why the boundary rule and the material difference are
non-negotiable rather than nice.

**Objection 2 — the room's hand makes Find look like chrome, and writers ignore chrome.**
**Answer:** that is the intent. Find *is* chrome — a dial, not a voice. Making it look
like an instrument is what stops it reading as a second mouth.

**Objection 3 — "sends nothing" is a claim, and claims decay.** A later ticket adds a
"smarter" Find and the promise quietly dies. **Answer:** this is the real risk, and prose
cannot hold it. **The deck already solved it and its solution is adopted whole:** the
button law is discharged *by proof rather than by prose* — the harness asserts zero
outbound calls of any kind on press, from both ends, and the assertion is proven by
falsification (a `fetch` planted in the press handler must turn the check red). A promise
that a mutation test defends is a promise that survives its authors.

### THE COGNITION BENCH

One row, one button, fixed position; results appear below without moving the row.
Nothing animates in. The row does not appear and vanish by context — it is present in the
panel wherever the panel is, so its position can be learned once.

## SECOND PASS — CANDIDATES

**FN1 · FIND IS A LOCAL INSTRUMENT AND ITS PRESS SENDS NOTHING.** Zero network on press,
**asserted from both ends and proven by falsification** — the deck's pattern adopted
whole. This assertion *is* Find's disclosure obligation, discharged by proof rather than
prose. (Nick's ruling; deck phase §5, §6.1.)

**FN2 · PLACEMENT — DIRECTLY BENEATH THE COMPOSER**, inside `Talk it through`, separated
by the panel's own rule; lenses below, unchanged. The rule marks the boundary between
what leaves the desk and what does not. (Nick's word.)

**FN3 · THE MATERIAL DIFFERENCE — FIND IS SET IN THE ROOM'S HAND (Figtree), the composer
in the writer's voice (Crimson Pro).** A Find query is an instrument setting, never the
writer's words travelling. This is the primary non-verbal signal.

**FN4 · FIND'S RESULTS NEVER ENTER THE MESSAGE STREAM.** Their own region beneath the
row, in the room's hand, each hit a button that **navigates** — the Fragments mechanic,
travel never insertion. No speaker, no bubble, no scroll into the conversation.

**FN5 · THE PLACEHOLDER NAMES A SCOPE, NOT A QUESTION.** Recommended: `Find in this
project…` against the composer's `Ask a question…`. The two must not rhyme.

**FN6 · ENTER IN FIND PERFORMS A FIND, AND NOTHING ELSE, EVER.** No keyboard path from
the Find input reaches the send path. Harness obligation, with a falsification test.

**FN7 · NO METER, NO COUNTER-CHROME.** A Find costs nothing and shows nothing at the
panel's foot; the desk does not propose a "this cost nothing" line. The results region's
own heading carries the instrument's nature so the meter's absence is confirmatory
rather than load-bearing.

**FN8 · SEARCH STAYS LOCAL, AND TAG AWARENESS IS A SEPARATE CHANNEL.** Nick's *"without
an AI check"* governs Find permanently. The Tutor's coming awareness of tags (item 108)
is a **different, consented channel** with its own button naming under candidate B, and
**must not be routed through this row.** If a future ticket proposes making Find
"smarter," that is a proposal to break FN1 and returns to Nick by name.

**OPEN — for Nick:** the placeholder's exact words (FN5) · whether Find's scope is the
project or the whole desk, and whether that scope is fixed or chosen — **the desk
recommends fixed at project scope for the first cut**, because a scope selector is a
third control in a row designed to be unmistakable.
