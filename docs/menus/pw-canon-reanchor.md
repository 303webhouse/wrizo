## §B · THREE OF MY CLAIMS ARE FALSIFIED. NAMED, NOT QUIETLY EDITED.

### B1 · "The board's canvas is a surface" — WRONG

**I wrote** (`pw-addendum-three-space-canon.md` §1): *"A Board is a container the writer can
also stand inside. **Its canvas is a surface** (you write cards, you draw ink)."*

**Nick, verbatim:** *"No writing or drawing is made on a board — it merely holds
cards/pages/imported docs."*

**Falsified.** The correct statement, which reaches PW13's same conclusion on sound
grounds: **a board is a container you stand inside to arrange what it holds; the writing
happens on the surfaces it holds, never on the board.** PW13 (the Plan tab is answered by
where you stand) survives unchanged — only my reason for it was wrong.

*Consequence worth naming: this is also why **item 124** opens. If no writing happens on a
board, the board's own text leg on the Tutor wire has nothing to read — and BD4's census
found it rides today.*

shape teaches the kind.

**Ruled (canon rider, confirmed):** *"PAGE CARDS ON A BOARD ARE ASPECT-LOCKED — CONFIRMED.
**Resizable by SCALE, never stretched.**"* — **reading (b).**

**Folded, and the ruling is better than my recommendation.** Aspect-lock keeps everything I
argued for — the page keeps a page's proportions, so **shape still teaches the kind (S13)

"no aspect lock" for page-pins**, which is the checkable change the builder needs.

never stretched*. Correction carried in the rewrite (§D).

This bears directly on the arc's central move, so I will not resolve it quietly.

- **Canon effect (1):** *"The rail's job clarifies: **CONTAINERS on the rail; a surface's own
  connections shown ON THE SURFACE.**"*

**Where the three agree:** a page's connected *boards* are containers, so listing them in

**Where they do not:** effect (5) puts **a board's contained surfaces in the COUNSEL**, and

list.

**Three readings, and I decline to pick:**

   one thing may have several), and it costs nothing: the Counsel's version is the reading
   hand, the rail's is the travelling hand.
2. **The rail wins for travel; the Counsel wins for reading about cards** (item 124's
   "the Counsel reads the contained cards" is about *content*, not navigation) — my own
   lean, because it gives each hand a distinct job rather than two copies of one.
3. **Effect (5) supersedes Q3/Q4**, and the side menu belongs on the right.

**Q19 for Fable/Nick.** Reading 2 is the only one where the writer never sees the same list
twice; reading 1 is the only one where neither ruling has to yield.
