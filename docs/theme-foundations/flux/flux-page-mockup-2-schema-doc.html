  • The page glitches when you are not writing. After a few idle seconds, one word at a time scrambles toward code and resolves back. It stops the instant you type. It is an overlay drawn over the text: the stored words never change.
  • The linked nodes open from the page. Each linked block has a square on the page's right edge. Click it: a line shoots out, opens into a box with the node(s) and what you can do with them, and an electron runs the line back to the page. Click the square again, the ×, or press Esc to close.

Jacked in

A burst mode for an ADHD mind: everything that could become a rabbit hole is gone, and the only direction is forward.

  • One line, at eye level. You write on a single bright line held at the same height. Everything else is a tunnel of signal rushing toward you, and it rushes faster the faster you write: speed you can feel, never a score.
  • No looking back. The line you just finished dims above; two lines up it compiles into unreadable signal. You cannot reread, so you cannot polish. Backspace works inside the word you are on and no further.
  • No exits by accident. No rail, no hands, no links, no glitch, no menus. Esc is the only way out.
  • A gentle floor. Stop for four seconds and the tunnel slows; a quiet line suggests "any word will do". Nothing is lost and nothing scolds.
  • A burst has an edge. A thin lime line drains across the top (ten minutes here). Jack out and the burst decompiles into readable text; add it to the doc as a new block, or send it to your logs.
