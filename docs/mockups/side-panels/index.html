The kettle clicks off and she still doesn’t turn around. Steam writes a short sentence on the window, then forgets it.

He could say her name. He doesn’t. Instead he lines the cups up by the sink, handles all facing the same way, the way their mother used to, and waits for the room to decide something.

Outside, the ferry sounds once, low, like a door closing somewhere far off. She counts the seconds before the second horn. There isn’t one.

“You kept the blue one,” she says, finally. Not a question.
